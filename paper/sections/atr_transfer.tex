\section{Annotated-Relation Stress Test: Pedestrian Groups}
\label{sec:atr-transfer}

\paragraph{Role in the evidence chain.}
The Chicago and NYC releases contain real rows but omit the operational
relation, so they cannot measure whether a feasible-world frontier contains the
realized world. Controlled data provide exact truth but may encode the method's
own assumptions. ATR fills the middle layer with DIAMOR pedestrian trajectories
and separately coded social groups \citep{atrgroupdata2015,zanlungo2014potential}.
We mask group membership from candidate scoring and retain it only to audit
whether a geometric support contains a real annotated relation. This is a
candidate-support stress test, not a claim of general external validity.

\paragraph{Release operator and scope.}
At each fixed snapshot, candidate dyads satisfy a distance radius
$r\in\{1,1.5,2,3,5\}$ meters and at most $90$ degrees of heading difference.
The annotated dyad count $m$ is disclosed but membership is hidden, yielding a
cardinality-$m$ matching frontier for mean within-dyad separation. This dyad
count is distinct from the selected-buffer count $q$ in the event model. Labels also
determine eligibility and exclusions, so the experiment is explicitly
oracle-assisted. The frozen protocol uses a two-minute grid; DIAMOR-1 is the
pilot and DIAMOR-2 the predeclared follow-up, not a pristine holdout. Full
annotation handling and unknown-dyad-count checks are in the appendix.

\paragraph{Coverage and support.}
All 850 radius--snapshot cells terminate with either replayed numerical MILP
optima (806 cells) or candidate-graph infeasibility (44 cells).  All 195 cells
within a 12-vertex limit also agree with an independent subset-recursion oracle.
Conditional on the annotated matching being representable, the frontier covers
the realized mean separation in all $769/769$ cells.  The closest-pair point
rule makes 68 threshold errors in those cells; every error occurs where the
frontier crosses the threshold. These are positive controls implied by
including the annotated world, not estimates of accuracy.

Candidate completeness is not automatic.  Across both days, a 1-meter radius
contains the annotated world in only 124 of 170 eligible snapshots; this rises
to 157 at 1.5 meters and 165 at 5 meters.  The gain comes at the cost of wider
identified sets: on the predeclared follow-up day, mean width rises from
$0.043$ meters at $r=1$ to $3.861$ meters at $r=5$.  At $r=5$, 410 of 495
valid-support threshold decisions (82.8\%) remain ambiguous.  Scalar aggregate
coverage under a misspecified graph is not relational coverage: the annotated
matching may be absent even when its scalar happens to lie inside the interval.
Here misspecified support produces 16 unflagged wrong point decisions across
eight snapshots. Removing oracle cardinality and evaluating a frozen learned
point rule across three queries preserve the same boundary: all false
certificates arise when candidate support omits the annotated world, and
similar edge recall does not imply similar decision reliability (appendix).

\paragraph{Calibrating candidate support across days.}
Finally, on DIAMOR-1 we search a fixed $8\times6$ radius--motion-angle grid,
require 95\% snapshot-level relational coverage, and select the qualifying
rule with the fewest mean candidate edges. The selected 3-meter, 120-degree
rule contains the annotated world in 85/88 pilot snapshots with 4.52 candidate
edges per snapshot. Frozen on DIAMOR-2, it covers 81/82 snapshots, but candidate
density rises to 12.17 edges per snapshot and only 29/246 threshold decisions
are certified. Coverage transfers in this comparison; sparsity and decision
informativeness do not.

A post-hoc mechanism audit then caps each node at its nearest incident edges
inside that frozen support. DIAMOR-1 selects $k=2$ as the sparsest cap preserving
the fixed rule's 85/88 pilot coverage. On DIAMOR-2 it preserves 81/82 coverage
and removes 80/998 candidate edges (8.0\%), yet certifies exactly the same
29/246 decisions. Simple degree control therefore reduces graph bulk without
restoring informativeness; structural alternative matchings survive the cap.
All 82 fixed graphs have positive width; 73 lower--upper witness pairs differ
through multiple, small alternating components. The cap leaves every lower
endpoint unchanged and contracts 21 upper endpoints, but crosses no evaluated
threshold. This is exactly the separation allowed by
Proposition~\ref{prop:k2thresholdcontraction}: graph compression need not yield
decision identification.

\paragraph{What ATR adds---and what it does not.}
The informative result is the failure boundary, not $769/769$ conditional
coverage. A plausible geometric rule can omit the joint world while leaving a
feasible optimization problem; widening support restores relational coverage
only by widening the frontier. ATR therefore confirms the support failure on a
separately collected annotation, while Chicago and NYC assess release-specific
scale and ambiguity. The labels are a one-coder annotated reference, not
error-free physical truth; the audit neither validates city candidate rules nor
estimates group prevalence. Research-use trajectories and detailed witnesses
are not redistributed.
