\section{Optimization and Its Scope}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{The two-member benchmark}
The restriction $K=2$ requires exactly two members per event; it is stronger
than simultaneous capacity $C=2$. Fix an admissibility graph with no
buffer--buffer edges. A graph-based audit uses this graph as its declared
model; equivalence to temporal worlds additionally requires that admissible
pairs can be realized jointly. This holds for fixed times, and for disjoint
pairs with independent row supports and no cross-event constraints.

\begin{theorem}[$K=2$ core-cover matching equivalence]
\label{thm:k2matching}
Admissible two-member event partitions correspond bijectively to matchings
that saturate every core. Fixing $q$ selected buffers fixes the number of
edges at $m=(|C_0|+q)/2$. Every pair-additive objective has the same value on
the partition and its matching. Its endpoints are minimum- and maximum-weight
core-saturating matchings with this cardinality.
\end{theorem}

This standard reduction supplies a baseline, not the general-event algorithm.
When timestamps are uncertain, the bijection is between partitions and
matchings; a partition can have many timestamp completions.

\begin{theorem}[Alternating decomposition of a pair-mean frontier]
\label{thm:k2alternating}
For $H(M)=m^{-1}\sum_{e\in M}w_e$, let $M^-,M^+$ attain the endpoints
at the same cardinality $m>0$. Their symmetric difference decomposes into
vertex-disjoint alternating paths and even cycles $\mathcal A$, and
\begin{equation}
 \overline H-\underline H
 =\frac1m\sum_{A\in\mathcal A}
 \left(\sum_{e\in A\cap M^+}w_e-
       \sum_{e\in A\cap M^-}w_e\right).
 \label{eq:alternating-width}
\end{equation}
Cycles are edge-count balanced. Path imbalances belong to $\{-1,0,1\}$ and
sum to zero, so imbalanced paths may require coupled exchanges.
\end{theorem}

For per-core queries, replace $m$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:alternating-width} by
$|C_0|$. The decomposition describes witness differences, not a new matching
algorithm.

\paragraph{Candidate-support sensitivity.}
Within a declared outer graph $E$, choose a base $E_0\subseteq E$ and let
$d_e=|e\cap C_0|\ind\{e\notin E_0\}$. Allow at most $\Gamma$ core
incidences through edges outside the base:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{e\in E}d_ex_e\le\Gamma.
 \label{eq:k2omission}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Nested candidate-omission frontier]
\label{prop:k2omission}
At fixed $q$, these feasible sets expand with $\Gamma$, from the base graph
at $0$ to the full graph at $|C_0|$. On nonempty sets, lower endpoints cannot
increase and upper endpoints cannot decrease.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Support contraction and threshold decisions]
\label{prop:k2thresholdcontraction}
For $E'\subseteq E$ with both fixed-$q$ sets nonempty,
$\underline H_E\le\underline H_{E'}\le\overline H_{E'}\le\overline H_E$.
An ambiguous threshold remains ambiguous exactly when
$\underline H_{E'}<\eta\le\overline H_{E'}$.
\end{proposition}

These are nesting consequences. Budget-constrained matching is solved
numerically; no extension of the polynomial matching claim to arbitrary
side constraints is implied. Nor does the budget cover relations outside $E$.

\subsection{Integral pricing for one fixed-time event}
Fix exact intervals, a core root $r$, and a span $[a,b)$ containing $I_r$.
Only intervals contained in this span may be selected. Sorted observed
endpoints induce elementary segments and internal boundaries. A selected
family has span $[a,b)$ and positive-overlap connectivity precisely when each
segment is covered and each internal boundary is strictly crossed by some
selected interval. Segment coverage is additionally bounded above by $C$.
Boundary crossing rules out components that only touch.

\begin{theorem}[Integral rooted single-event oracle]
\label{thm:oracle}
With rational endpoints and additive weights, and integer capacity,
the fixed-span coverage, boundary-crossing, capacity, and variable-bound
formulation is integral. Fixing a core root and a distinct companion preserves
integrality. Enumerating observed endpoint spans and companions gives a
polynomial-time minimum- or maximum-weight single-event oracle.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Argument.}
Interleave segment and boundary rows in temporal order. Each interval column
has consecutive ones. Lower and upper bounds use signed copies of these rows;
root, companion, and exclusion constraints are variable fixes. Total
unimodularity and integral right-hand sides imply integral vertices. The
companion enforces event size at least two without adding an arbitrary
cardinality constraint. There are $O(|V|^2)$ spans and at most $|V|-1$
companions per root. The appendix gives the formulation and proof.

\subsection{From local pricing to global support maximization}
Let $\R_C$ contain every feasible fixed-time event and
$b_R=|R\cap B_0|$. The full event-column relaxation is
\begin{align}
\max_{\lambda\ge0}\quad &\sum_{R\in\R_C}b_R\lambda_R
\label{eq:masterobj}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad &\sum_{R\ni i}\lambda_R=1 &&i\in C_0,
\label{eq:coremaster}\\
&\sum_{R\ni j}\lambda_R\le1 &&j\in B_0.
\label{eq:buffermaster}
\end{align}
Given core duals $\alpha_i$ and nonnegative buffer duals $\beta_j$, a
column's reduced gain is
$b_R-\sum_{i\in R\cap C_0}\alpha_i-\sum_{j\in R\cap B_0}\beta_j$.
It is additive, so Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle} supplies pricing over all core
roots. An artificial-core phase establishes LP feasibility.

\begin{proposition}[Full-master LP certificate]
\label{prop:cg}
If a feasible restricted master is optimal and exhaustive exact pricing
finds no positive reduced gain, its value equals the full-master LP optimum.
\end{proposition}

Local integrality does not imply an integral master: the appendix exhibits
$\operatorname{OPT}_{LP}=4>3=\operatorname{OPT}_{IP}$ at capacity two.
Solving an integer model on root-LP columns alone is therefore insufficient.

\paragraph{Integer closure.}
\label{sec:bp}
Branch-and-price first branches on fractional buffer usage. When usages are
integral but event columns are fractional, Ryan--Foster branching puts two
active rows together or in separate events. Pricing expands these restrictions
into exhaustive forced-in/forced-out cases, each handled by the fixed-span
oracle. Case counts can grow exponentially with branch depth.

\begin{proposition}[Integer certificate]
\label{prop:bp}
If node relaxations are closed by exact compatible pricing, disjunctions are
exhaustive, and the finite search terminates, it certifies the full integer
support optimum or infeasibility. At interruption, a verified incumbent $L$
and valid node bounds $U_n$ give
$L\le M(\T,C)\le\max\{L,\max_{n\in\mathrm{open}}U_n\}$.
\end{proposition}

A node with unfinished pricing retains an inherited valid bound; its
restricted-master value is not a full-master upper bound. This result concerns
fixed-time support maximization, not arbitrary aggregate queries or a
polynomial-time global algorithm.

\subsection{Set-valued timestamps and numerical status}
With uncertain time, membership and one common timestamp completion must be
chosen jointly. Substituting full outer intervals can add false bridges or
artificial concurrency; the appendix gives both counterexamples. The
continuous MILP handles rectangular start/end supports and uses a declared
positive overlap/length margin $\epsilon$,
and certificates refer to that formulation. It need not coincide with all
strictly positive overlaps of arbitrarily small duration. We distinguish
replayed feasible witnesses, numerical optimality, model infeasibility, and
unresolved termination. A witness proves feasibility but not an endpoint;
a timeout proves neither optimality nor infeasibility.
