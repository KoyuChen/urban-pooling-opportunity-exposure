\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What is identified.}
The method identifies sharp or certified endpoints only relative to the
declared candidate universe, timestamp supports, connectivity rule, capacity,
and query. It does not identify a unique partition. The strongest empirical
statement is comparative: expanding time supports or capacity cannot shrink a
fixed-support feasible set, and the audits quantify how much selected support
and outcome ranges move under those declared relaxations.

\paragraph{Algorithmic boundary.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle} makes the single-run pricing problem tractable, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:cg} certifies the full LP relaxation. The exact integer
master remains open. Branch-and-price is the natural next step, but branching
must preserve a solvable pricing problem. A complementary goal is a hardness or
fixed-parameter boundary in capacity, temporal width, or core count.

\paragraph{Candidate support and validation.}
A true run omitted before optimization cannot be recovered by endpoint
certification. Future work should pair broad public support with ground-truth
or semi-synthetic recall tests, then scale the predeclared panel to many more
cohorts. Point-reconstruction baselines remain useful only if evaluated by
whether their downstream answers lie inside the certified frontier and how
often they fail under known truth.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}

Neither city release exposes a public vehicle-run key. We therefore do not
claim actual co-riders, partner recall outside the candidate universe,
production matching logic, realized pool size, or vehicle capacity. The
15-minute NYC supports are an artificial information-loss experiment, not an
assertion about the TLC observation operator. The six-window panel is too small
and purposive for a citywide prevalence estimate or causal effect. A true run
omitted from the declared candidate universe cannot be recovered by endpoint
certification, and numerical optimality remains conditional on the recorded
solver status and tolerances.

The unit is a released transaction, not a stable person. Public zones and
neighborhood attributes describe context, not individual socioeconomic status
or preferences. Publishing extremal partner assignments could create
reidentification risk, so the artifact suppresses row identifiers and latent
world witnesses and reports only aggregate results and audit metadata.

\section{Generative AI Usage}
Generative AI assisted drafting, code review, counterexample search, and
editing. All mathematical claims, empirical outputs, citations, and artifact
states remain author-verified; generated text is not treated as city evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
Public rows can be individually rich yet relationally incomplete. We formulate
hidden shared events as ordered, bounded-occupancy interval runs with exact or
set-valued timestamps and propagate their ambiguity to aggregate queries. The
resulting geometry separates timestamp support, capacity, and candidate
support; the algorithm separates an integral single-run pricing problem from a
nonintegral cross-run master. Chicago and NYC audits show that second-level
time does not identify shared-run membership and that timestamp uncertainty can
produce capacity-equivalent support expansion whose magnitude varies by
regime. The next decisive step is an exact integer branch-and-price method and
a larger predeclared validation panel, not a more confident point linkage.
