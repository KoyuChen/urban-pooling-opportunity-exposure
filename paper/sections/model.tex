\section{Latent Ordered-Event Worlds}
\label{sec:model}

\paragraph{Released rows and boundary roles.}
Let $V=C_0\cup B_0$ be a finite candidate universe. Core rows $C_0$ define the
target cohort. Buffer rows $B_0$ lie near its temporal boundary and may belong
to an event containing a core row, but need not be selected. This distinction
prevents an arbitrary observation window from forcing every nearby row into
the target population.

For row $i$, the release supplies a timestamp support
\[
\T_i\subseteq\{(s,e):s<e\}.
\]
Exact public time is the singleton case. A latent completion chooses
$t_i=(s_i,e_i)\in\T_i$ for every row. We use half-open intervals
$I_i=[s_i,e_i)$ and define positive overlap by $|I_i\cap I_j|>0$.

\paragraph{Ordered temporal events.}
For a completed timestamp vector $t$, a subset $R\subseteq V$ is a feasible
event at simultaneous capacity $C$ if:
(i) $R$ contains at least two rows and at least one core;
(ii) the graph on $R$ joining positive-overlap pairs is connected; and
(iii)
\begin{equation}
 \max_{u}\sum_{i\in R}\ind\{u\in I_i\}\le C.
 \label{eq:capacity}
\end{equation}
Condition~\eqref{eq:capacity} limits simultaneous occupancy, not total event
cardinality. Thus one event may contain more than $C$ rows through sequential
turnover. In the ride-pooling audit such an event is a latent vehicle run; in
other streams it may be a referral episode, household visit, or service tour.

A world $F=(t,\Pi)$ consists of a timestamp completion and a collection of
disjoint feasible events such that every core row belongs to exactly one event
and every buffer row belongs to at most one. Unselected buffers remain outside
$\Pi$. Let $q(F)$ denote the number of selected buffers. For fixed $q$, write
\[
 \F(\T,C,q)=\{F:q(F)=q\}.
\]
The reachable support set and maximum are
\[
 Q(\T,C)=\{q:\F(\T,C,q)\ne\varnothing\},\qquad
 M(\T,C)=\max Q(\T,C).
\]

\paragraph{Aggregate functionals.}
Let $H(F)$ be any event-label-invariant aggregate computable from public row
attributes and the latent partition, such as mean outcome among selected
buffers, within-event dispersion, exposure, or event count per core. For
nonempty $\F(\T,C,q)$ define
\begin{equation}
 \underline H(\T,C,q)=\min_{F\in\F(\T,C,q)}H(F),\quad
 \overline H(\T,C,q)=\max_{F\in\F(\T,C,q)}H(F).
 \label{eq:endpoints}
\end{equation}
Both extrema are attained for the finite fixed-time models used below. The
continuous-support implementation reports a feasible witness, certified
infeasibility, or unresolved status rather than coercing a timeout.

\begin{proposition}[Support and capacity nesting]
\label{prop:nesting}
Suppose $\T_i\subseteq\T'_i$ for every row and $C\le C'$. Then, for every $q$,
\[
 \F(\T,C,q)\subseteq\F(\T',C',q).
\]
Consequently $Q(\T,C)\subseteq Q(\T',C')$ and
$M(\T,C)\le M(\T',C')$. For fixed $q$ and any $H$,
\[
 \underline H(\T',C',q)\le\underline H(\T,C,q),\qquad
 \overline H(\T',C',q)\ge\overline H(\T,C,q).
\]
\end{proposition}

The fixed-$q$ condition matters. Comparing outcomes at each capacity's own
maximum $M(\T,C)$ changes both the world set and the conditioning event; those
ranges need not widen monotonically.

\paragraph{Capacity-equivalent information loss.}
For fine support $\T$ and coarser support $\T'\supseteq\T$, define
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_{\T\to\T'}(C)=
 \min\{k\ge0:M(\T,C+k)\ge M(\T',C)\}.
 \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
This measures how many fine-time capacity steps are required to reach the
support attainable under coarse time. When a coarse solver only certifies a
feasible lower bound $L(\T',C)\le M(\T',C)$, replacing $M$ by $L$ yields a
conservative lower bound on the comparison, not a sharp equality.
