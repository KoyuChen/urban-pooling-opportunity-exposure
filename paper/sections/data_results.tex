\section{Public-Data Audit Design}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Complementary releases.}
Chicago's TNP release provides a declared two-transaction shared-trip benchmark
with nearest-15-minute public times and privacy-coarsened geography
\citep{chicagodata2026,chicagomanual2026,chicagoprivacy2026}. NYC's High Volume
For-Hire Vehicle release provides second-level pickup and drop-off times,
shared-match indicators, Taxi Zones, trip miles, and trip time, but no public
co-rider or run key. We use the official 2023 Open Data table\footnote{Dataset
\texttt{u253-aew4}: \url{https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/u253-aew4}.} for
audited exact optimization and the May 2026 monthly Parquet only for current
candidate-multiplicity scale. These datasets instantiate a general
relation-incomplete event problem; neither supplies ground truth for operational
event membership.

\paragraph{Visible candidate universes.}
A candidate rule is part of the estimand rather than hidden preprocessing.
Chicago uses a boundary-complete core-incident temporal universe and reports
radius and omitted-incidence sensitivity separately. NYC uses provider and
temporal proximity; exact Taxi Zone equality is only a sensitivity screen
because it can delete plausible relations. No learned score or public outcome
enters the headline candidate construction.

\paragraph{Certification levels.}
The experiments are certification audits, not population estimation.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item A current-month NYC count audit measures shared-match rows and candidate
multiplicity for a fixed 200-row cohort.
\item A fixed 2023 pairwise cohort contains 38 cores and 399 buffers and
compares exact seconds with artificial 15-minute outer intervals.
\item Ordered-event audits use exact or set-valued time and
$C\in\{2,3,4\}$. A common-support cohort has eight cores; a separately
predeclared replication panel uses four cores and 12 outcome-blind temporal
buffers in each of six windows.
\item A four-core, 12-buffer exact-time cohort compares complete event-column
enumeration, column generation, and exact branch-and-price. The same cohort is
used only as a small algorithmic audit, not as a city-scale claim.
\end{enumerate}
The six panel windows were frozen before their frontiers were inspected. They
span weekday morning, weekday evening, weekend evening, and three seasons, but
remain purposive rather than probabilistically sampled.

\paragraph{Solver and disclosure contracts.}
Exact fixed-time support frontiers use complete enumeration where declared.
Column generation closes a full LP only after every rooted pricing problem is
solved and no improving column remains. Branch-and-price calls an integer
optimum only after the node queue closes; otherwise it retains an incumbent and
open-node upper bound. Continuous existential-time cells use solver
certificates and witness replay, and unresolved counts are never classified as
infeasible. Artifacts publish aggregate tables, hashes, and statuses, but no
raw identifiers, event columns, partner assignments, or latent timestamps.

\section{Public-Data Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Fine timestamps do not identify the hidden relation}
The May 2026 NYC file contains 222,149 public shared-match rows. In a fixed
200-row cohort, exact public interval overlap yields 154,708 directed
core-candidate incidences over 1,042 candidate nodes; the median core degree is
786. Requiring equal pickup and drop-off Taxi Zones leaves only 44 incidences.
Zone equality is therefore an aggressive candidate screen, not a defensible
necessary condition for hidden co-membership.

Table~\ref{tab:pairwise} uses the separate fixed 2023 pairwise cohort. Exact
seconds already leave broad ranges. Artificial 15-minute time coarsening adds
79.5\% more candidate edges and increases the three displayed identified
widths by 12.4--15.3\%. Time coarsening aggravates ambiguity but does not create
it.

\subsection{Capacity uncertainty is front-loaded}
To isolate the pure effect of capacity, Table~\ref{tab:capacity} fixes the same
72 selected buffers across $C=2,3,4$. Proposition~\ref{prop:nesting} then
guarantees nested feasible sets. Miles width nearly doubles from $C=2$ to 3
but rises only 6.0\% from $C=3$ to 4; duration behaves similarly. In this
cohort, 89.2\% of the total miles widening and 86.2\% of the duration widening
from $C=2$ to 4 occurs at the first relaxation. This is a cohort-specific
identification result, not an estimate of realized physical capacity.

A different conditioning event explains an initially counterintuitive pattern.
At each capacity's own maximum support, composition ranges can contract because
larger $C$ forces inclusion of more rows rather than permitting an extremal
subset. Holding support fixed restores the monotone set comparison. This
separates the extensive margin---which rows can enter some feasible
world---from the conditional composition margin.

\subsection{Timestamp support and capacity are partially substitutable}
Table~\ref{tab:panel} reports the six-window existential-time panel. Every
window has a positive certified coarse-support gain at both $C=2$ and $C=3$.
The $C=2$ gain ranges from one to seven buffers. Artificial coarse $C=2$
reaches the exact $C=3$ maximum in two of six windows, whereas coarse $C=3$
reaches the exact $C=4$ maximum in all six. The latter comparison is capped by
the 12-buffer design, so it indicates support substitution within the declared
panel, not universal saturation.

The defensible conclusion is not that a 15-minute support is always ``one
extra seat.'' Timestamp information and simultaneous capacity are partially
substitutable dimensions of the identified set, with a magnitude determined by
local interval density and chain structure.

\subsection{Exact decomposition needs few LP columns and closes the IP}
Table~\ref{tab:bp} uses one exact-time public cohort. At $C=4$, complete
enumeration contains 1,719 feasible event columns, while 102 columns
(5.9\%) close the full LP. Solving the integer problem requires columns that
may not be generated for the root LP alone: the restricted integer objectives
at $C=3,4$ are seven and ten, below the full optima eight and 12. The
branch-and-price layer closes both values while preserving exact pricing at
every node.

The public cohort has essentially zero root integrality gap, so it does not by
itself show why branching is necessary. The locked nonintegrality witness does:
its root LP is four, its integer optimum is three, and branch-and-price closes
the value after 17 nodes. Together, the two audits distinguish column economy
from integer difficulty. They establish an exact medium-instance algorithmic
path, not a city-scale runtime guarantee.
