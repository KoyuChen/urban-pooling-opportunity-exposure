\section{Public-Data Audit Design}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Complementary releases.}
Chicago's TNP release provides a declared two-transaction shared-trip benchmark
with nearest-15-minute public times and privacy-coarsened geography
\citep{chicagodata2026,chicagomanual2026,chicagoprivacy2026}. NYC's High Volume
For-Hire Vehicle release provides second-level pickup and drop-off times,
shared-match indicators, Taxi Zones, trip miles, and trip time, but no public
co-rider or run key. We use the official 2023 Open Data table\footnote{Dataset
\texttt{u253-aew4}: \url{https://data.cityofnewyork.us/d/u253-aew4}.} for
audited small-cohort optimization and the May 2026 monthly Parquet only for
current candidate-multiplicity scale.

\paragraph{Candidate universes.}
A candidate rule is deliberately visible. Chicago uses a boundary-complete
core-incident temporal universe and reports radius and omitted-incidence
sensitivity separately. NYC uses provider and temporal proximity; exact Taxi
Zone equality is only a sensitivity screen because it can delete plausible
partners. No learned score enters the headline results.

\paragraph{Audit levels.}
The experiments are designed as certification gates rather than population
estimation.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item A current-month NYC scale audit counts shared-match rows and candidate
multiplicity for a fixed 200-row cohort.
\item A fixed 2023 pairwise cohort contains 38 core and 399 buffer rows and
compares exact seconds with artificial 15-minute outer intervals.
\item Ordered-run audits use exact or set-valued times and $C\in\{2,3,4\}$.
One common-support cohort has 8 cores; a predeclared replication panel uses 4
cores and 12 outcome-blind nearest temporal buffers in each of six windows.
\item A 4-core, 12-buffer exact-time cohort compares complete run-column
enumeration with column generation.
\end{enumerate}
The panel windows were fixed before their support frontiers were inspected.
They span weekday morning, weekday evening, weekend evening, and three seasons.
They are purposive, not a probability sample.

\paragraph{Certification and disclosure.}
Exact fixed-time support frontiers use complete run-column enumeration.
Continuous existential-time cells use solver certificates and witness replay;
unresolved counts are never classified as infeasible. Published numerical
endpoints have zero reported MIP gap. Artifacts contain aggregate tables,
counts, hashes, and statuses, but no raw trip identifiers, selected run columns,
partner assignments, or latent timestamp witnesses.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Fine timestamps do not identify the hidden relation}
The May 2026 NYC file contains 222,149 public shared-match rows. In a fixed
200-row cohort, exact public interval overlap yields 154,708 directed
core-candidate incidences over 1,042 candidate nodes; the median core degree is
786. Requiring equal pickup and drop-off Taxi Zones leaves only 44 incidences,
showing why zone equality cannot be treated as a necessary partner rule.

Table~\ref{tab:pairwise} uses the separate fixed 2023 pairwise cohort. Exact
seconds already leave broad ranges. Artificial 15-minute time coarsening adds
79.5\% more candidate edges and increases the three displayed identified
widths by 12.4--15.3\%. Time coarsening aggravates ambiguity but does not create
it.

\subsection{Capacity uncertainty is front-loaded}
To isolate the pure effect of capacity, Table~\ref{tab:capacity} fixes the same
72 selected buffers across $C=2,3,4$. The feasible sets are then nested by
Proposition~\ref{prop:nesting}. Miles width nearly doubles from $C=2$ to 3 but
rises only 6.0\% from $C=3$ to 4. Duration width behaves similarly. In this
cohort, 89.2\% of the total miles widening and 86.2\% of the duration widening
from $C=2$ to 4 occurs at the first relaxation. This is a cohort-specific
identification result, not an estimate of realized vehicle capacity.

buffers.}

\small

A different conditioning event explains an initially counterintuitive pattern.
If each $C$ is evaluated at its own maximum support, the selected-buffer
composition range contracts with capacity because larger $C$ forces the world
to include more rows rather than cherry-pick extreme outcomes. Holding support
fixed reverses that visual contraction exactly as nesting predicts. This
separates an extensive margin---which rows can enter any feasible world---from
a conditional composition margin.

\subsection{Timestamp uncertainty and capacity are partially substitutable}
Table~\ref{tab:panel} reports the six-window existential-time panel. Every
window has a positive certified coarse-support gain at $C=2$ and $C=3$. The
gain varies substantially, from one to seven buffers at $C=2$. Artificial
coarse $C=2$ reaches the exact $C=3$ maximum in two of six windows, whereas
coarse $C=3$ reaches the exact $C=4$ maximum in all six. The latter comparison
is capped by the 12-buffer audit design, so it is evidence of support
substitution within the declared panel, not universal saturation.

The correct conclusion is therefore not that 15-minute uncertainty is always
``one extra seat.'' Rather, timestamp information and simultaneous capacity are
partially substitutable dimensions of the identified set, with a magnitude
determined by local interval density and chain structure.

\subsection{Exact pricing closes the LP with few columns}
Table~\ref{tab:cg} compares column generation with complete enumeration on one
exact-time cohort. At $C=4$, the number of feasible run columns rises to 1,719,
but 102 columns---5.9\%---suffice to certify the full LP optimum. The restricted
integer master is lower than the complete integer optimum at $C=3$ and 4,
matching the theoretical nonintegrality boundary. Column generation is thus a
useful LP engine and lower-bound generator, not yet an exact integer solver.
