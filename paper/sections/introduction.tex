\section{Introduction}

Many data releases preserve rows while deleting relationships. A stream may
report each encounter's time, location, duration, and outcome but suppress the
key that says which rows belonged to the same referral episode, household
visit, field-service tour, shared ride, or other bounded-capacity event.
Analysts can still count transactions, but event-level composition, exposure,
dispersion, utilization, and threshold decisions depend on an unobserved
partition. A clustering or linkage algorithm returns one plausible partition;
it does not by itself establish that the downstream conclusion is invariant to
the other partitions that remain compatible with the release.

We call such streams \emph{row-complete and relation-incomplete}. Their central
knowledge-discovery problem is not always to guess the missing relation.
Sometimes the scientifically relevant question is instead:
\begin{quote}
Which aggregate conclusions survive every latent event world allowed by the
released information and explicitly stated structural assumptions?
\end{quote}
This distinction matters whenever a point reconstruction is used to trigger a
policy threshold, rank subpopulations, or compare operating regimes. A point
estimate may be feasible and reproducible yet still sit on the wrong side of a
decision boundary relative to another equally feasible world.

Ride pooling provides a demanding public audit instance, but not the scope of
the framework. A released row may state that a trip was successfully shared
without exposing a co-rider or vehicle-run key. High-capacity events are not
pairwise cliques: $A$ may overlap $B$, $B$ may overlap $C$, and $A$ may never
overlap $C$, while all three belong to one sequential run whose simultaneous
occupancy is two. The same ordered-event structure appears when cases,
customers, or visits enter and leave a shared episode over time. Timestamp
coarsening creates a second ambiguity. Treating the full public envelope as the
realized interval is generally wrong because it can simultaneously create
artificial overlap and artificial congestion. The correct
partial-identification quantifier asks whether \emph{there exists} a latent
exact timestamp completion inside every released support.

We introduce \methodname, a certification framework for aggregate queries on
such event streams. A visible candidate universe is divided into required core
rows and optional boundary buffers. A feasible world chooses exact intervals
inside released timestamp supports and partitions selected rows into connected,
bounded-occupancy temporal events. Rather than outputting a preferred graph, the
method minimizes and maximizes a downstream functional over all feasible worlds.
Every displayed endpoint is accompanied by an attained witness internally or by
an explicit solver status; unresolved computations are not converted into
infeasibility. The resulting interval is conditional on the declared candidate
universe, supports, connectivity rule, and capacity. It is not a frequentist
confidence interval, nor does it claim that every interior scalar is attainable.

This paper makes four contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt,topsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Identification geometry.} We formalize core--buffer ordered-event
worlds with set-valued timestamps, positive-overlap connectivity, occupancy
capacity, and aggregate functionals. We prove joint nesting under timestamp
support expansion and capacity relaxation and define a capacity-equivalent
information-loss index.
\item \textbf{Structure-exploiting optimization.} For one fixed-root,
fixed-span event, interleaved segment-coverage and boundary-bridge rows have the
consecutive-ones property. This gives an integral LP oracle for additive event
pricing and an exact Dantzig--Wolfe certificate for the full master relaxation.
\item \textbf{Certified integer decomposition.} The cross-event master is not
integral. We therefore branch first on optional-row usage and then on
Ryan--Foster pair co-membership. Each branch is priced through a finite family
of forced-in/forced-out versions of the same interval LP. Exhausting the branch
queue certifies the global integer support optimum; timeouts retain valid lower
and upper bounds.
\item \textbf{Validation and public audits.} In 3,000 controlled-truth
instances, we evaluate point reconstructions, threshold decisions, and
candidate truncation separately from city evidence. Chicago and NYC then expose
complementary release regimes and separate hidden-linkage, timestamp-support,
and capacity ambiguity without publishing reconstructed links.
\end{enumerate}

The controlled study shows why certification changes the knowledge-discovery
answer. Conditional on the true support count but not membership labels, a
feasible temporal point rule makes the wrong decision at predeclared thresholds
in 17.4--19.0\% of cases across capacities two through four. Every error occurs
when the certified frontier crosses the threshold, so \methodname{} correctly
refuses to present that decision as relation-invariant. Candidate completeness
is a distinct gate: keeping roughly 84\% of true members leaves the full true
event world representable only 31--33\% of the time, although a nonempty
frontier at the true support count usually still exists. Row-level recall can
therefore substantially overstate relational coverage.

The public audits show that fine timestamps and feasible point outputs do not
resolve the missing relation. A 24-window exact-second NYC design yields 21
eligible windows and 126 outcome--capacity cells. At the candidate-median
threshold, 125 cells are certified ambiguous and one remains unresolved; four
feasible deterministic point methods nevertheless output 498
capacity-indexed decisions, 494 of them inside ambiguous cells, and disagree
with one another in 24 cells. A separate scale lattice closes all three
capacities through 8 core plus 24 buffer rows and 14 of 18 cells overall; the
remaining cells retain global gaps of one to four. In Chicago, a
snapshot-stable sharded extraction count-closes 60 cores, 611 temporal
candidates, and 50,405 endpoint-bin contributors. Its positive-length outer
time-envelope graph admits two distinct core covers that differ on all 60 core
assignments, but this is graph-cover multiplicity rather than a completed pair
of operational hidden-run worlds. Thus increasingly exact computation sharpens
the boundary between what is and is not relation-invariant; it does not invent
missing identities.
