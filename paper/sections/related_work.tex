\section{Related Work and Positioning}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Forward allocation is not our estimand.}
Shareability and dynamic-pooling models begin with observed requests and ask
how a platform should allocate them. Santi et al. connect pairwise feasibility
to maximum matching \citep{santi2014shareability}; Alonso-Mora et al. enumerate
feasible groups for online trip--vehicle assignment
\citep{alonsomora2017ondemand}; and Ozkan and Ward study forward-looking
matching with heterogeneous arrivals and patience
\citep{ozkanward2020dynamic}. Related operations work shows that market
thickness can reduce matching under search frictions
\citep{linetessine2020thickness} and that ride-sharing density economies can
produce spatially unequal access \citep{ghili2026access}. We do not propose
another allocation policy or assume that thickness improves matching. We solve
the inverse problem created when request outcomes remain public but the
realized grouping is suppressed.

\paragraph{Node prediction is not our contribution.}
Fair ride-pooling objectives redistribute service or driver income in an
observed matching problem \citep{raman2021fairness}. Chicago studies establish
that pooling demand and match outcomes vary systematically across trips and
neighborhoods. Soria and Stathopoulos relate disadvantage to pooled and solo
demand in 127 million trips \citep{soria2021sociospatial}; Taiebat, Amini, and
Xu predict sharing requests and successful matching
\citep{taiebat2022sharing}; Mucci and Erhardt separate the sharing request from
the subsequent match outcome \citep{mucci2024understanding}; and Sebti and
Chen combine spatiotemporal clustering with match classifiers
\citep{sebtichen2026hidden}. These papers establish that node behavior is
predictable. A node classifier by itself is not a contribution here. Our
contribution begins when those outcomes must be reconciled with a globally
feasible but unobserved pairing and their uncertainty carried into exposure.

\paragraph{Policy volume effects are not exposure effects.}
Chicago's differentiated transportation-network-company tax already has a
causal literature. Abkarian, Hegde, and Mahmassani report a shift toward shared
trips after the January 2020 differential against a declining baseline
\citep{abkarian2023taxing}; Zheng et al. find lower overall ridership, growth
in shared trips, decline in solo trips, and spatial heterogeneity
\citep{zheng2023congestion}. Those results concern counts and shares. Our
planned intention-to-treat analysis asks whether a policy-induced change in
the authorized pool changes the admissible composition of cross-neighborhood
opportunities. Because the groups are suppressed, that outcome must be
optimized over feasible pairings rather than computed from a preferred graph.

\paragraph{Opportunity exposure is not social contact.}
Carpooling has been related to cross-racial relational costs
\citep{charleskline2006carpooling}, while mobility studies distinguish
residential location from the composition of visited places
\citep{wang2018isolation,moro2021mobility}. Later work constructs
person-to-person exposure networks \citep{nilforoshan2023mobility}, documents
an efficiency--mixing tension in local consumption
\citep{abbiasov2024fifteen}, and shows that replacing self-reported income
with home-neighborhood income can mechanically lower measured mixing
\citep{fan2026hidden}. Chetty et al. separate exposure from tie formation
conditional on exposure \citep{chetty2022socialcapital2}. Chicago's public TNP
data contain neither stable persons nor verified co-presence, and ACS income
is a neighborhood attribute rather than rider income. We therefore estimate
admissible-set opportunity exposure, not realized contact, preference
homophily, or an echo chamber.

\paragraph{Point linkage is not our output.}
Sadinle treats record linkage as globally constrained bipartite matching and
quantifies uncertainty over admissible links \citep{sadinle2017linkage}. Our
setting has one event file, no observed co-rider edge, and weak labels on nodes
rather than labeled record pairs. Physical constraints define the untrimmed
feasible set; weak supervision supplies only a declared score restriction; and
optimization propagates the surviving ambiguity into the downstream exposure
functional. AI is useful only when it tightens that set without defeating
known-truth coverage, not because it produces a point-imputed graph.
