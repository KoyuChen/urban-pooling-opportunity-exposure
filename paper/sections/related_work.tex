\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Uncertain linkage and aggregate queries.}
Globally compatible record linkages and downstream uncertainty are established
objects. Sadinle models bipartite linkage as a constrained matching
\citep{sadinle2017linkage}; Hua and Pei evaluate aggregate queries over
compatible probabilistic linkages \citep{hua2012aggregate}; and Turkcapar and
Krishnan compute extremal aggregate answers over candidate-constrained
two-table linkages \citep{turkcapar2023aggregate}. Attribute-annotated uncertain
databases propagate value bounds through relational queries
\citep{feng2021audb}. Our target difference is conditional and narrower: a
within-file nonbipartite relation and endpoint attributes are simultaneously
hidden, while global release counts can couple their completions. When
attribute supports are independent, our problem collapses to ordinary weighted
matching; that case is a baseline, not a novelty claim.

Classical cell-suppression work solves disclosure-control problems under
linear table constraints by integer programming and branch-and-cut
\citep{fischetti2001suppression}. Our inverse object is different: released
threshold states constrain latent micro-labels that also determine a hidden
matching. Integer programming remains a general fallback, not an algorithmic
novelty.

\paragraph{Resource-constrained matching and structured inference.}
An additive score floor introduces the same second resource studied in
standard budgeted matching, which is NP-hard and admits PTAS/EPTAS algorithms
\citep{berger2011budgeted,doronarad2023eptas}. We do not assume those schemes
transfer to mandatory core cover, and a generic score constraint or
approximation scheme is not our contribution. Likewise, bounded-width constraint processing and bucket
elimination are standard \citep{freuder1990ktree,dechter1999bucket}. Global
side constraints must be compiled before width is assessed: rainbow matching
remains APX-complete even when the underlying edge-coloured graph is a path
\citep{le2014rainbow}. Our algorithmic increment is an executable temporal
state for this release-coupled world model, with active factor lifetimes,
threshold caps, score-resource tracking, and endpoint witnesses.

\paragraph{Weak supervision and calibrated restrictions.}
Multiple-instance learning recovers instance-level structure from bag labels
only through modeling assumptions \citep{maron1998mil}. In our application,
node match outcomes do not nonparametrically identify partner-edge rankings,
so we treat any edge model as a scorer. Split conformal calibration supplies
finite-sample marginal coverage under exchangeability
\citep{lei2018conformal}. More directly, Cauchois et al. develop conformal
prediction with weak supervision for structured outputs and instantiate
bipartite perfect matchings, partial-matching feedback, and matching-oracle
implementations \citep{cauchois2024weak}. Thus neither matching-level conformal
prediction nor a matching oracle inside calibration is new here. Our
calibration markets reveal the complete matching; we use rank calibration as
a safety layer for a release-coupled nonbipartite optimizer. Candidate and
release-set eligibility remain separate assumptions.

\paragraph{Ride pooling and contextual composition.}
Shareability networks and dynamic assignment solve a forward allocation
problem with observed requests \citep{santi2014shareability,alonsomora2017ondemand}.
Chicago work predicts sharing and matching outcomes
\citep{taiebat2022sharing,mucci2024understanding}, relates pooled demand to
neighborhood disadvantage \citep{soria2021sociospatial}, and constructs proxy
groups by spatiotemporal clustering \citep{sebtichen2026hidden}. Our flagship
application asks an inverse measurement question: among a precisely defined
set of realized two-transaction matches, which neighborhood-context
compositions remain compatible with the suppressed partner key and the
verified release operator? It does not infer rider socioeconomic status,
preferences, durable ties, or an echo chamber.
