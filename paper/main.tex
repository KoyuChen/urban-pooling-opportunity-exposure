\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Urban pooling platforms use prediction and combinatorial optimization to
decide which requests can share a vehicle. A thicker pool may create more
cross-neighborhood opportunities, or it may add matches mainly within already
similar parts of a city. Thickness need not even raise aggregate matching when
search frictions grow with market size \citep{linetessine2020thickness}, and
ride-sharing density economies can distribute service access unevenly across
space \citep{ghili2026access}. Our outcome is different from both match rate
and access. The scientific question is whether additional feasible matches
broaden or narrow the socioeconomic composition of opportunity exposure.

The public observation operator makes that question nonstandard. Chicago's
Transportation Network Provider records report sharing authorization,
realized match status, and \texttt{trips\_pooled} for each trip, but suppress
the service-chain identifier, vehicle identifier, provider, and stable rider
identity. Declaring spatiotemporally nearby rows to be co-riders would turn
privacy coarsening into false certainty. Dropping the relational object would
leave a node-level match-rate regression that cannot measure exposure. We take
a third route: characterize the set of groupings consistent with the released
records and propagate the remaining ambiguity into a service-chain
opportunity estimand, not a claim of verified co-presence.

\methodname{} performs three operations. It first screens candidate edges using
declared temporal, origin--destination, and directional constraints. It then
learns compatibility scores from node-level match labels with a noisy-OR
likelihood; no pair label is created, and an edge score is not a posterior
probability that two riders shared a vehicle. Finally, it minimizes and
maximizes the mixing statistic over globally feasible exact-cover pairings.
The untrimmed physical set is the coverage reference. A score-retention floor
is a model-indexed sensitivity restriction, not a confidence interval.

Completed evidence establishes the method's boundary, not a Chicago policy
sign. On the locked known-truth generator, the candidate graph
covers all hidden pairs, the primary score tightens the interval while
retaining truth, and a diagnostic score with geography-equality features
creates false precision by excluding truth at a stringent retention level.
The production design is locked to complete Chicago market days and the
January 6, 2026 policy change; the prefix extract supplies only a schema and
feasibility audit. Complete-day latent-group estimation, exposure intervals,
and policy signs are reserved for the production extract and are not claimed
in this draft.

The paper makes four contributions. First, it formulates suppressed pooling
groups as structured missing relations rather than a clustering target.
Second, it maps node-level match outcomes into edge scores without
manufacturing pair labels. Third, it reports optimization-based admissible
intervals for opportunity exposure and audits the coverage cost of model-based
tightening. Fourth, it provides a reproducible, human-experiment-free policy
design whose data, estimand, diagnostics, and claim gates are fixed in advance;
this is a design contribution, not a policy conclusion.

\section{Data and Scientific Setting}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Chicago TNP records}

The City of Chicago's 2025-- TNP release contains one record per customer trip
\citep{chicagodata2026} and includes start
and end timestamps rounded to 15 minutes, trip distance and duration, pickup
and drop-off census tracts or community areas, centroid coordinates, fares and
charges, plus authorization, match, and pooled-chain-size fields. The last
field, \texttt{trips\_pooled},
counts all customer trips from the time the vehicle was empty until it became
empty again; two component riders need not have been simultaneously present.
Each component has a separate row and the same count, but the common chain ID
is suppressed. Accordingly, our pair estimand is a service-chain compatibility
opportunity, not physical co-presence.

\subsection{What the observation operator preserves}

The release does not merely add noise to a conventional supervised-learning
table. It preserves outcomes on individual rows while deleting the relational
key that would join those rows. Table~\ref{tab:observation-operator} separates
what each field licenses from the stronger interpretation it cannot support.
This distinction fixes the analysis unit before modeling: trips are observed
nodes, two-trip service chains are latent exact-cover pairs, and every reported
exposure statistic is conditional on a declared set of feasible joins.

Two sources of selection remain visible even before latent pairing. Sharing
authorization determines who can enter the pool, and geographic suppression
determines whose neighborhood context can enter $H$ or $D$. We therefore keep
authorization/matching outcomes on their full eligible denominators, restrict
the primary exposure statistic to pair-eligible rows with joined origin
geography, and report the resulting support loss by treatment cell. Neither
complete-case analysis nor a learned score repairs non-random suppression; both
only define a transparent target population.

A deterministic two-character trip-ID-prefix sample covers December 9, 2025
through February 2, 2026 and contains 53,241 trips, including 3,048 authorized
and 2,146 matched records. It is suitable for schema tests and aggregate-rate
feasibility, but not latent-edge reconstruction: conditional on one component
being sampled, its counterpart is omitted with probability approximately
255/256 under the sampling device. Structural estimation therefore requires
complete narrow day slices. The completed audit finds 70.41\% matched among
authorized trips, zero matches without authorization, and 83 matched records
with \texttt{trips\_pooled}$<2$. Pickup tracts and centroids are missing for
38.05\% and 8.98\% of rows; the corresponding drop-off rates are 38.39\% and
9.21\%. Conditional on release, 95.24\% of pickup and 94.99\% of drop-off
tracts join to the ACS file. These quantities describe this prefix extract,
not the Chicago population.

\subsection{Socioeconomic proxies}

We join pickup census tracts to the 2024 five-year American Community Survey
Detailed Tables B19013 and B03002 \citep{censusacs2024}.
The prepared extract contains 1,551 Chicago-area tracts and supplies median
household income and race/ethnicity composition. Income and composition refer
to the trip's origin neighborhood, not to the rider. The primary pair statistic
uses ACS income quintiles; continuous sensitivity analyses use absolute log
income differences. Rows without a released and joined pickup tract remain in
match-outcome estimation but not in the primary SES bound.

\subsection{Policy setting and completed feasibility signal}

Chicago changed its congestion-zone surcharge structure on January 6, 2026
\citep{chicagotax2026}. Inside the new zone, the solo--shared surcharge gap is
\$0.90 during weekday active hours and \$1.50 on weekends.
Our planned estimand is the policy intention-to-treat effect on authorization,
successful pooling, and the opportunity-exposure interval, using a
post-by-zone-by-active-hours design. The policy changes both market thickness
and selection into the shared pool, so thickness is a mechanism, not an
excluded instrument.

In the prefix feasibility sample, the approximate newly covered zone during
weekday active hours shows authorization increasing from 5.95\% to 8.08\% and
matched/all trips from 4.61\% to 6.89\%. Mean additional charges increase from
\$5.69 to \$6.31 in the treated cell. These are unadjusted go/no-go signals,
not causal estimates. Complete-day rows, not this prefix, are required for the
candidate graph and policy contrasts. Appendix~\ref{sec:aux-validation} records
the raw feasibility cells, frozen extraction protocol, data status, and an NYC
temporal-coarsening stress test.

\section{Weakly Supervised Structured Inference}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Target: exposure over latent pairings}

The target is a statistic of feasible pairings, not a reconstructed social
network. Let $V=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ index authorized trips in a complete
market-day slice. Trip $i$ has released features $x_i$, node match label
$y_i\in\{0,1\}$, released service-chain size $k_i$, pickup-neighborhood log
income $Z_i$, and predeclared income bin $B_i$. The suppressed object is a set
$M$ of mutually non-conflicting pairs. For the primary pair analysis, we retain
$V_1=\{i:y_i=1,k_i=2\}$ with valid geography and require every retained node
to appear exactly once. If $m=|V_1|/2$, the target statistics are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 H(M)&=m^{-1}\!\sum_{\{i,j\}\in M}\ind\{B_i=B_j\},\\
 D(M)&=m^{-1}\!\sum_{\{i,j\}\in M}|Z_i-Z_j|.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:estimands}
\end{equation}
$H$ is the share of pairs from the same pickup-neighborhood income bin, not
degree-corrected network assortativity; $D$ is a continuous sensitivity
outcome. Both describe neighborhood-level opportunity exposure. Neither the
pairing nor verified co-presence is observed.

\subsection{Feasibility: candidate construction}

A candidate edge says only that two records could have belonged to the same
two-trip service chain; it is not an inferred co-rider link. We admit an
undirected edge $(i,j)$ only within a calendar day and train/test split when the
start-time gap is at most 30 minutes, pickup and drop-off distances are at most
4 and 7 km, and OD-vector cosine is at least $-0.25$. A normalized
spatiotemporal nearest-neighbor query proposes at most 96 records. Physical
cost ranks these proposals, and a deterministic cap limits degree to 16. The
surviving edges form $E_C$.

The production-primary feature map $\phi_{ij}$ has 22 columns covering time,
OD, duration, mileage, direction, interval overlap, squares, exponentials, and
predeclared interactions. A 28-column diagnostic map adds six
same-tract/same-area indicators and interactions. Neither map uses endpoint
match status, \texttt{trips\_pooled}, fare, or ACS SES. Search and degree caps
can delete the true edge, so every result is conditional on $E_C$. We report
support, degree, and threshold sensitivity; true-edge recall is observable only
in synthetic validation.

\subsection{Likelihood: node-label weak supervision}

The public label says whether a trip matched, not which incident edge was
realized. We therefore learn edge compatibility only through a bag-level node
likelihood. For candidate edge $(i,j)$, let
$a_{ij}=\phi_{ij}^{\top}\theta$ and $q_{ij}=\sigma(a_{ij})$. Incident edges
form a bag for node $i$ and induce the noisy-OR probability
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \widehat p_i(\theta)
  &=1-\prod_{j:(i,j)\in E_C}(1-q_{ij})\\
  &=1-\exp\!\left\{-\sum_{j:(i,j)\in E_C}
      \operatorname{softplus}(a_{ij})\right\}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:noisyor}
\end{equation}
On candidate-supported training nodes $\mathcal T$, we estimate
\begin{equation}
 \widehat\theta\in\arg\min_\theta
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal T|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}
 \{-y_i\log\widehat p_i-(1-y_i)\log(1-\widehat p_i)\}
 +\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta_{-0}\|_2^2,
 \label{eq:mil-objective}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda=10^{-3}$, an unpenalized intercept, stable softplus evaluation,
and L-BFGS-B optimization. The fitted $q_{ij}$ is a compatibility score inside
this working likelihood. It is neither an observed edge label nor a calibrated
posterior probability that two riders shared a vehicle.

The learned model is linear over the fixed nonlinear basis above. A transparent
rule uses the same physical dissimilarities with disclosed fixed weights; only
its intercept and nonnegative global scale are fitted by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:mil-objective}. Whole days form the train/test split. The
likelihood therefore supports node prediction only. Public-data pair accuracy
is undefined; synthetic truth is used only after fitting to audit candidate
recall and within-node edge ranks.

\subsection{Optimization: exact-cover exposure bounds}

The scientific output is the range of $H$ or $D$ over all exact-cover pairings
consistent with the declared graph, not the model's highest-scoring pairing.
For candidate edge $e$, let $z_e\in\{0,1\}$ indicate selection, let $w_e\ge0$
equal the fitted $q_e$ (or the comparator's rule score), and let $g_e$ be an SES statistic such as
$\ind\{B_i=B_j\}$ or $|Z_i-Z_j|$. The admissible exact covers are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal M(E_C)=\{z\in\{0,1\}^{|E_C|}:\;&
  \sum_{e\ni i}z_e=1\quad \forall i\in V_1,\\[-2pt]
  &z_e=0\quad\text{if }e\not\subseteq V_1\}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:matching}
\end{equation}
Every feasible solution selects $|V_1|/2$ edges, so the untrimmed admissible
interval is
\begin{equation}
  \left[\frac{2}{|V_1|}\min_{z\in\mathcal M(E_C)}\sum_e g_ez_e,
        \frac{2}{|V_1|}\max_{z\in\mathcal M(E_C)}\sum_e g_ez_e\right].
 \label{eq:bounds}
\end{equation}

AI enters only as a declared sensitivity restriction. Let
$W^*=\max_{z\in\mathcal M(E_C)}\sum_e w_ez_e$ and define
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal M_\rho(E_C)=
 \left\{z\in\mathcal M(E_C):\sum_e w_ez_e\ge\rho W^*\right\}.
 \label{eq:rho-set}
\end{equation}
Replacing $\mathcal M(E_C)$ by $\mathcal M_\rho(E_C)$ in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounds} yields a score-restricted interval. Here $\rho$ is an
analyst-chosen sensitivity parameter, not a confidence level, and the resulting
interval is not a confidence interval. All programs are binary linear programs
solved with HiGHS and reported only with a global optimality certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional sharpness and coverage]
\label{prop:coverage}
If $\mathcal M(E_C)$ is nonempty and both extrema are solved globally,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:bounds} is sharp over the declared candidate graph. If the
suppressed true pairing $z^\dagger$ belongs to $\mathcal M(E_C)$, the untrimmed
interval contains its target value. The same membership-based guarantee holds
for a score-restricted interval exactly when
$z^\dagger\in\mathcal M_\rho(E_C)$, equivalently
$\sum_e w_ez_e^\dagger\ge\rho W^*$.
\end{proposition}

Both feasible sets are finite, so certified extrema are attained and bracket
every member. Moreover,
$\mathcal M_{\rho_2}(E_C)\subseteq\mathcal M_{\rho_1}(E_C)$ for
$\rho_2\ge\rho_1$: increasing $\rho$ can narrow the interval, but it can also
discard the true pairing. Failure of the score floor removes the structural
coverage guarantee; it need not numerically exclude the same scalar target.
The appendix gives the formal proof.

\subsection{Interpretation and audit logic}

The construction compares two nested admissible sets. Conditional on the
exact-cover cohort and field consistency, $\mathcal M(E_C)$ additionally
requires the physical screen to contain every retained true pair. It does not
assume that a learned score ranks that pair highly. The restricted set
$\mathcal M_\rho(E_C)$ adds one claim: an
admissible pairing must retain at least a declared fraction of the best total
compatibility score. Reporting both sets makes the source of sharpening
visible. If the raw interval is wide, the public observation operator leaves
substantial structural ambiguity. If only the score-restricted interval is
narrow, the apparent information comes from the model and must survive a
known-truth coverage audit.

Exact-cover optimization is essential because edge decisions are coupled. A
high-scoring edge consumes both endpoints, changes which other edges remain
available, and changes the composition of a complete pairing.
Thresholding edges independently, clustering by proximity, or taking the
maximum-score pairing would answer a different question: which single graph an
algorithm prefers. Our min/max programs instead ask which exposure values
remain possible after every retained matched node is assigned exactly once.
They can return a wide range or infeasibility, and either outcome is part of the
scientific result.

The validation targets the sequence of claims created by this construction.
Candidate recall tests whether truth enters $E_C$. Held-out Brier loss tests
whether edge scores aggregate to calibrated node matchability. Hidden-edge
ranking tests whether good node prediction orders the unobserved relation in a
known-truth market. Finally, true-pairing eligibility tests whether the score
floor preserves the member whose target generated the synthetic data. Only the
first and last checks support structural coverage; node prediction alone does
not. In Chicago, where pair truth is unavailable, we can report node
calibration, support, feasibility, and sensitivity across declared graphs, but
we cannot turn those diagnostics into empirical pair accuracy.

These intervals also differ from sampling confidence intervals. The admissible
range conditions on one released market slice and varies the suppressed
relation. Sampling uncertainty instead varies market slices or dates. The
complete analysis will recompute structural endpoints inside date-block
resamples and will display the untrimmed range, the family indexed by $\rho$,
and sampling uncertainty separately. No choice of $\rho$ is calibrated to have
90\% or 95\% frequentist coverage.

\section{Identification Conditions and Falsification}
\label{sec:identification}

Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage} is deliberately conditional. It does not turn
privacy-coarsened records into observed relationships; it states what follows
after the candidate graph, exact-cover cohort, and optional score floor are
declared. Table~\ref{tab:identification} makes those conditions operational and
preassigns the interpretation of their failure. This prevents a favorable
point estimate or narrow interval from overruling a failed measurement check.

\begin{table*}[t]
  \caption{Identification conditions, diagnostics, and retained claims.}
  \label{tab:identification}
  \small
  \centering

    \midrule

    Contextual target validity & ACS release/join rates, suppression strata, continuous-gap and area-level alternatives & Claim is limited to the observed neighborhood-context subpopulation \\

    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}

Candidate completeness is the strongest structural requirement and the least
directly testable in Chicago. Search windows and degree caps make computation
possible, but either can delete a true pair. High node support is therefore not
proof of high true-edge recall: a node can have many physically plausible
neighbors while its actual service-chain partner is absent. We use known-truth
markets to measure recall and use Chicago support, component structure, and
threshold sensitivity to reveal fragile regions. The resulting raw range is
sharp over $E_C$, not over every relationship that could exist under a looser
physical model.

Exact-cover feasibility provides a different diagnostic. An infeasible
component can reflect an overly narrow candidate rule, inconsistent public
chain-size fields, or eligibility decisions that remove a required counterpart.
It is not repaired by matching the largest feasible subset, because doing so
changes both the population and the exposure denominator. Production results
therefore report infeasibility by date and socioeconomic support stratum. Any
revised candidate graph is run as a separately declared sensitivity analysis,
not chosen because its exposure estimate is more precise.

Score alignment is an additional assumption, not a consequence of feasibility.
The noisy-OR likelihood rewards node-level prediction after aggregating all
incident edges. It can succeed by learning candidate degree, market density, or
a geographic shortcut while ordering the true partner poorly. This is why the
validation reports Brier loss, hidden-edge rank, and true-pairing eligibility as
distinct outcomes. If a score restriction fails the known-truth audit, a narrow
range is evidence about the model's preference, not stronger identification.

Finally, $H$ and $D$ inherit the meaning of their contextual labels. They
compare released pickup-neighborhood income, not rider resources or preferences.
A high same-bin share may reflect residential sorting, trip geography, platform
assignment, or chance within a constrained feasible set. Without persons,
repeated ties, or beliefs, it cannot establish preference homophily or an echo
chamber. The urban contribution is narrower: it asks whether the algorithmically
mediated opportunity set is compatible with more or less cross-neighborhood
exposure under explicit observation and model restrictions.

\section{Validation: Sharpening and False Precision}
\label{sec:results}

Before the first held-out run, we fixed a two-day Chicago-coordinate generator
with 80 hidden pairs and 80 unmatched authorized trips per day, 15-minute time
rounding, model defaults, and score retentions $\rho\in\{0.90,0.95\}$. Pair IDs
and synthetic income bins were stored in separate truth files and never read by
candidate construction, model fitting, or score-restricted optimization. Day
one is used for fitting; day two is evaluated once.

\paragraph{Candidate support comes first.}
The holdout graph retains all 80 hidden pairs. This check is logically prior to
prediction: if a true pair were absent, even the untrimmed interval would lose
the coverage guarantee in Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage}. Across a separate
20-replicate implementation check, candidate recall and untrimmed scalar coverage are
100\% at 1-, 5-, 15-, and 30-minute rounding; Appendix~\ref{sec:aux-validation}
reports the full coarsening table.

\paragraph{Node calibration does not imply edge recovery.}
On 240 candidate-supported holdout nodes, the rule has Brier loss 0.06048,
compared with 0.00595 for the 22-feature primary score and 0.00513 for the
28-feature diagnostic score. Both learned scores therefore look excellent on
the objective they were trained to predict. Hidden-edge ranks separate them.
The primary score raises mean reciprocal rank from 0.781 to 0.941 and top-1
accuracy from 67.5\% to 90.0\%. The diagnostic score instead falls to 0.775 and
64.4\%. Better node prediction is not evidence of better pair recovery; no
edge label enters training.

\noindent\textbf{Eligibility and containment, not width alone, audit sharpening.}\par\noindent
The hidden same-income-bin share is 0.5625. Physical admissibility alone gives
$[0.050,0.775]$. Score retention narrows this set, but
Table~\ref{tab:bounds-results} shows that width cannot be read without coverage.
The primary score covers the hidden value and retains the complete hidden
pairing at both score floors. The diagnostic $\rho=0.95$ region is only 0.162 wide but
lies above the hidden value and excludes the hidden pairing. Even scalar
containment is a weak check: the rule's $\rho=0.95$ interval happens to contain 0.5625
although the complete hidden pairing fails its score floor.

\paragraph{Geography equality manufactures precision.}
The diagnostic map adds six same-tract and same-area columns without changing a
single candidate edge: both models optimize over the same 2,640-edge graph.
Among the 560 candidates incident to matched holdout nodes, pickup- and
drop-off-tract equality coincide exactly with the synthetic same-income-bin
target, while area equality is constant. The diagnostic model can therefore
improve its node objective while sorting pairings by a proxy for the evaluation
outcome. Removing those columns worsens node Brier only from 0.00513 to 0.00595,
improves edge ranking, and restores coverage. This is feature-induced false
precision, not proof that the primary map is proxy-free: continuous coordinates
may still encode socioeconomic geography.

At $\rho=0.90$, the primary interval is slightly wider than the rule interval
(0.475 versus 0.438); at $\rho=0.95$ it is slightly narrower (0.300 versus
0.312) and uniquely retains the hidden pairing. The pilot validates the
coverage audit and the possibility of safe sharpening. It does not establish
that AI uniformly tightens a transparent rule.

\paragraph{The prefix extract is a negative software check.}
The incomplete Chicago prefix graph contains 151 edges and supports only 246 of
3,048 authorized nodes (8.1\%). On 93 supported holdout nodes, weak MIL has
Brier loss 0.15777 versus 0.15525 for the rule. Under approximately independent
prefix retention, a two-trip chain's other component is absent with probability
255/256. We therefore neither tune the graph nor run exact-cover bounds on this
extract. Complete-day Chicago support, calibration, exposure ranges, and policy
contrasts remain reserved for the predeclared extraction.

\section{Complete-Day Policy Design}
\label{sec:policy}

Chicago changed its congestion-zone surcharge on January 6, 2026. We estimate
an intention-to-treat contrast comparing post-policy trips in the covered zone
during active hours with the corresponding spatial and temporal controls.
Outcomes are authorization among eligible trips and successful pooling among
all eligible trips. The surcharge can change both the size and composition of
the shared pool; market thickness is therefore a mechanism, not an excluded
instrument. Event-time leads and the charge discontinuity must validate timing
and pre-trends before causal language is used.

The complete-day analysis distinguishes a volume response from a composition
response. Authorization/all trips measures entry into the eligible shared pool;
matched/all trips measures successful pooling without conditioning on that
post-policy choice. Candidate support and exact-cover feasibility are then
audited on the same spatial and temporal cells used for exposure. A discontinuity
in supported composition at the policy boundary would be a measurement change,
not evidence of mixing. The design therefore requires stable support and
parallel pre-trends in addition to a visible surcharge first stage.

For opportunity exposure, regressing estimated daily lower endpoints does not
generally produce a lower bound on a policy coefficient. Let $\mathcal F_c$
denote the admissible pairings in market-time block $c$ and let $a_c$ be the
fixed weights of the policy contrast. We propagate latent-edge ambiguity
through the contrast itself:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \underline\theta&=\min_{M_c\in\mathcal F_c\ \forall c}
     \sum_c a_c H(M_c),\\
 \overline\theta&=\max_{M_c\in\mathcal F_c\ \forall c}
     \sum_c a_c H(M_c).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:effect-bounds}
\end{equation}
Date-block resampling layers sampling uncertainty on this structural range
while holding the candidate rule fixed.

Equation~\eqref{eq:effect-bounds} prevents an invalid shortcut. The lower
endpoint on each date can be produced by a different latent pairing, and the
weights $a_c$ can be positive or negative. Regressing a sequence of daily lower
endpoints therefore need not minimize the policy contrast. Optimizing the
contrast jointly over all block-specific pairings preserves the declared
admissible set. If this joint range crosses zero, the direction of the exposure
effect is not identified even when one point reconstruction has a stable sign.

The analysis has three falsifiable hypotheses. \textbf{H1 (thickness)} predicts
a positive ITT on authorization/all and matched/all. \textbf{H2 (opportunity
exposure)} is two-sided: the policy range in Eq.~\eqref{eq:effect-bounds} may
move toward cross-neighborhood exposure or toward geographically segmented
pooling. Its result is the sign of the full range, not the sign of one imputed
network. \textbf{H3 (AI value)} is a complete-day gate: relative to the rule at
the same score floor, the learned score must improve held-out node Brier loss by
at least 10\% and reduce interval width by at least 25\% without changing the
conclusion across the declared candidate grid. The synthetic benchmark audits
this gate and its failure mode; it does not claim that H3 has been met. Failure
of H3 leaves the rule and untrimmed ranges primary and does not invalidate H1.

\section{Discussion}

The urban object here is neither residential segregation nor a realized social
network. It is the set of cross-neighborhood service-chain opportunities that
could have generated the public records. This object matters because a pooling
algorithm can alter who is combinatorially available to whom before any durable
tie or attitudinal change occurs. A thicker market may widen that set across
neighborhoods, or it may add feasible pairs inside geographically segmented
submarkets. The direction is an empirical property of the admissible exposure
range, not a consequence of higher match volume.

The methodological distinction is the treatment of privacy suppression as a known
observation operator on relations. Conventional node prediction leaves the
scientific outcome undefined; point linkage replaces missingness with one
untestable graph. Here physical restrictions define a score-free set, and weak
supervision can only remove members through an explicit sensitivity rule. This
turns an otherwise hidden modeling choice into an auditable comparison between
the untrimmed and restricted ranges. The same pattern applies whenever public
data preserve object-level outcomes but suppress groups, assignments, or
encounters.

The locked failure is as important as the locked gain. Geography equality
improves the trained node objective and produces a much narrower interval, yet
at $\rho=0.95$ it removes the true pairing and the true target. Thus predictive
fit cannot certify relational identification. The useful AI contribution is
conditional sharpening with visible coverage cost: candidate support, node
calibration, hidden-edge ranking where truth exists, and structural eligibility
must be read together. Without complete-day Chicago data, this remains a method
and audit result rather than a finding about homophily or urban mixing.

\subsection{Implications for urban AI and market design}

The AI4Sciences role is not to replace a hidden graph with a more elaborate
prediction. It is to state how a learned restriction changes a scientific
identified set. The observation operator, physical candidate rule, node
likelihood, score floor, and exposure objective are separate objects; each can
be inspected or varied without silently redefining the others. This modularity
creates a falsifiable AI contribution. A model adds information only when its
restricted range is narrower, its predictive diagnostics improve out of day,
and known-truth audits do not show that the narrowing comes from excluding the
data-generating relation. Otherwise the scientifically relevant output is the
score-free range or a declaration of non-identification.

This framing also separates two operations questions that are often collapsed.
Market thickness concerns how many requests are available and whether more of
them match. Opportunity composition concerns which feasible assignments
account for those outcomes. A policy can raise authorization and successful
pooling while leaving a wide exposure range, or while shifting every admissible
pairing toward more same-neighborhood opportunities. Conversely, match volume
may change little while the composition of feasible pairs changes. The policy
ITT therefore applies to both entry and assignment opportunity; thickness is a
measured mechanism, not an exclusion restriction that isolates dispatch.

The method produces an evidence hierarchy for the eventual Chicago analysis.
A one-signed untrimmed policy range would be the strongest result available
from the declared candidate graph because it does not depend on learned edge
weights. A one-signed restricted range paired with an untrimmed range that
crosses zero would be conditional evidence: the conclusion follows only for
pairings sufficiently compatible with the stated score and $\rho$. Agreement
between the rule and primary model would show robustness to two score maps, not
truth of either map. Disagreement, loss of true-pairing eligibility in
simulation, or sign changes across candidate graphs would downgrade the claim
to model sensitivity. These categories are fixed before the policy outcome is
observed.

There is also a practical distinction between auditing and optimization. The
reported interval can reveal that a platform's public outcomes are compatible
with sharply segmented or broadly mixed opportunities. It does not identify
the proprietary objective, and it does not recommend forcing cross-neighborhood
pairs. An operational intervention would also have to account for detour,
waiting, reliability, price, driver burden, and rider choice---quantities absent
from the released table. The admissible-set analysis is therefore an input to
oversight, not a standalone welfare objective.

\subsection{Transfer and scope}

The same structure appears when administrative data preserve item-level
outcomes but suppress teams, referral chains, shipments, or shared resources.
Transfer requires a domain-specific feasibility screen and group constraint;
the ride-pooling thresholds are not portable. The general lesson is narrower:
when the missing scientific object is relational, validation should test
candidate support and downstream set coverage, not only node prediction. A
learned score may sharpen the answer, but the score-free feasible set must
remain visible as the reference against which that sharpening is judged.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}
\label{sec:ethics}

This study uses public, privacy-coarsened administrative records and aggregate
census statistics; it recruits no participants and performs no intervention.
The applicable institutional determination will be obtained and disclosed
before submission. Neighborhood income is not rider income, a candidate edge
is not verified co-presence, and \texttt{trips\_pooled} can join components of
one service chain that were never simultaneously occupied. Without a stable
rider identifier, the data cannot measure durable homophily, repeated exposure,
belief reinforcement, or an echo chamber. Non-random geographic suppression,
timestamp rounding, and ACS join failure can also change the analyzed
subpopulation and are reported by treatment cell.

Publishing one reconstructed graph could expose identities or stigmatize
neighborhoods. Released artifacts therefore contain aggregate intervals,
synthetic examples, and code, but no row-level inferred social graph. Score
restrictions can under-cover, so the untrimmed range and sensitivity grid remain
visible. The bounds are an auditing device, not a license to infer sensitive
attributes or optimize mixing at the expense of wait, detour, surveillance, or
disparate service burdens. If the effect range overlaps zero or changes sign
across declared graphs, the conclusion is non-identification.

\section{Generative AI Usage}
\label{sec:genai}

Generative AI tools (OpenAI ChatGPT and Codex; exact model identifiers and
access dates to be completed at submission) were used to assist code
scaffolding and debugging, literature-search organization, prose drafting, and
formatting. All research questions,
estimands, model restrictions, analyses, source verification, and claims remain
the responsibility of the authors. AI-generated code was executed against
deterministic tests and known-truth simulations; numerical results in the paper
are produced by saved scripts rather than generated text. No confidential
participant data or proprietary platform records were supplied to a generative
AI system for this draft. Generative AI did not recruit or interact with
participants, label empirical observations, or appear as an author. The final
disclosure will enumerate every material use across the full research process.

\section{Conclusion}

The missing object in privacy-coarsened pooling records is the relation, not
another trip feature. \methodname{} therefore reports an admissible exposure
interval rather than one reconstructed graph: physical constraints define the
exact-cover set, weak supervision supplies an optional score restriction, and
optimization carries the surviving ambiguity into the estimand. The locked
benchmark shows why AI is a tightening device rather than a discovery device.
A geography-equality-free score can narrow the range while retaining the hidden
pairing, whereas a shortcut-rich score looks sharper and excludes truth. The
complete-day Chicago intervals and policy sign remain reserved. This pilot is
evidence for the audit, not evidence that ride pooling changes urban social
mixing.

\subsection{Policy and failure protocol}

Let $Post_t$ indicate trips after January 6, $Zone_i$ the released spatial
proxy for the covered zone, and $Active_t$ the policy's active hours. The
frozen trip-level specification is
\begin{equation}
  Y_{ict}=\alpha_c+\delta_t+
  \beta(Post_t\times Zone_i\times Active_t)+
  \Gamma'X_{ict}+\varepsilon_{ict}.
  \label{eq:ddd}
\end{equation}
Event-time coefficients, zone-specific trends, and weekday/weekend dose
contrasts accompany Eq.~\eqref{eq:ddd}. Zone polygons and a boundary ambiguity
band are fixed before outcomes are estimated. Diagnostics include lead
equivalence tests, placebo dates, alternative spatial cells, holiday and venue
exclusions, and the surcharge discontinuity in
\texttt{additional\_charges}. Date-block resampling or a predeclared
small-cluster correction is chosen after auditing independent cells.

The candidate grid varies the 30-minute start window to 15 and 45 minutes,
pickup radii 4 km to 2 and 6, drop-off radii 7 km to 4 and 10, degree cap 16 to
8 and 32, and $\rho\in\{0,0.80,0.90,0.95\}$. No graph is selected after
observing exposure. Candidate support, component sizes, degree, exact-cover
feasibility, calibration by degree, and geographic suppression are reported by
date and treatment cell. Continuous-geometry and ACS-permutation ablations test
remaining proxies. Known-truth simulations cross coarsening, route overlap,
assortativity, and candidate misspecification; both scalar containment and true
pairing eligibility are recorded. A time-limit incumbent is never an endpoint.

Predeclared failures remain results. A partial day stops structural estimation;
an infeasible cell stays infeasible; a missed Brier/width gate leaves the rule
and untrimmed ranges primary; unstable candidate support is a measurement shift;
failed pre-trends imply descriptive association; and a policy-effect range that
crosses zero implies an unidentified direction. None is replaced by a
maximum-score pairing.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:coverage}}

The feasible binary vectors in $\mathcal M(E_C)$ are in one-to-one
correspondence with exact-cover pairings on the declared graph. This set is
finite, so certified minimization and maximization attain sharp endpoints and
bracket every feasible $z^\dagger$. The same argument applies to
$\mathcal M_\rho(E_C)$; the true pairing remains a member exactly when
$\sum_e w_ez_e^\dagger\ge\rho W^*$. Finally,
$\rho_2\ge\rho_1$ implies
$\mathcal M_{\rho_2}(E_C)\subseteq\mathcal M_{\rho_1}(E_C)$, which proves the
nested-endpoint claim without implying that truth remains feasible or that
scalar containment fails whenever membership does.

\section{Implementation Pseudocode}

\subsection{Degree-capped candidate graph}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Split by date/train--test assignment, project centroids, and build the
  normalized start-time/pickup-coordinate $k$-d tree.
  \item Query 96 neighbors, apply hard filters, deduplicate by physical cost,
  and retain at most 16 proposals per node.
  \item Forbid cross-date/split edges and emit support and degree diagnostics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Out-of-day weak edge scoring}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Construct compatibility features without labels, fares, or SES, then
  fit the rule intercept/scale on supported training nodes.
  \item Fit $\widehat\theta$ with the node likelihood and no edge label.
  \item Report held-out calibration; rank edges only in known-truth simulation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Certified exposure range}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item Verify graph integrity, finite scores, parity, and feasibility.
  \item Solve $W^*=\max_{z\in\mathcal M(E_C)}\sum_e w_ez_e$ to global
  optimality; add the score floor only for a declared $\rho$ analysis.
  \item Solve both SES extrema, reject uncertified incumbents, normalize by
  pair count, and retain certificates/configuration.
\end{enumerate}
