\title{Hidden Runs, Visible Rows: Certified Aggregate Frontiers under Timestamp and Capacity Uncertainty}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Public event streams often retain one row per transaction while suppressing the
key that joins rows into a shared event. In ride-pooling data, this leaves trip
outcomes visible but co-rider and vehicle-run membership hidden. We introduce
\methodname, a partial-identification framework that optimizes aggregate
queries over every temporally ordered latent-run decomposition consistent with
released rows, timestamp supports, and a declared simultaneous-capacity bound.
A run need not be a clique: sequential members may form an $A$--$B$--$C$ chain
even when $A$ and $C$ never overlap. We prove monotonicity of reachable support
and fixed-estimand endpoints under timestamp-support expansion and capacity
relaxation. For a fixed root and temporal span, interval connectivity and
occupancy admit a consecutive-ones formulation, giving an exact integral LP
pricing oracle. Dantzig--Wolfe column generation then certifies the full
fixed-time master LP, while an explicit counterexample shows that the integer
master remains nonintegral. Two public-data audits illustrate the geometry.
Chicago provides a declared two-transaction benchmark; NYC provides second-level
timestamps but unknown latent-run membership. Across six predeclared NYC audit
windows, artificial timestamp uncertainty expands certified reachable support
at capacities two and three in all six windows. On one exact-time cohort,
column generation closes the $C=4$ LP after generating 102 of 1,719 feasible
run columns. These are conditional feasible-world statements: we do not
recover partners, vehicle runs, realized capacity, or a population effect.
\end{abstract}
