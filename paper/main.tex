\title{Certified Temporal Frontiers for Aggregate Queries over Hidden Matchings and Privacy-Coarsened Attributes}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Public releases often preserve one row per transaction while suppressing the
key that joins rows into events and coarsening sensitive endpoint attributes.
Downstream aggregate queries then depend jointly on a latent relation and the
attributes attached to its endpoints. We optimize a query over feasible worlds
that jointly assign a nonbipartite core--buffer matching and categorical labels
subject to released supports and threshold-count factors. Independent label
supports collapse to weighted matching; coupled worlds need not. We develop an
exact temporal-frontier dynamic program whose state retains only live labels,
matching bits, active capped-count automata, an omitted-edge budget, and a
capped integer score resource. It returns attained lower and upper endpoints
with witness worlds. With $w$ live records, compiled support $d$, at most $r$
active threshold factors of cap $k$, omission budget $\Gamma$, and integer
score target $b$, the live-key bound is
$(2d)^w[3(k+1)]^r(\Gamma+1)(b+1)$. The dependence on $b$ is substantive:
the associated threshold decision problem is weakly NP-complete
even on disjoint four-cycles of pathwidth two. A downward score quantization
gives certified outward query endpoints while relaxing the original score by
at most $\eta|C|$; it is a bicriteria certificate, not a query FPTAS. We use
matching-level split conformal prediction as a known safety layer, with one
score normalization frozen across the entire candidate-omission path. A
locked benchmark matches every applicable exhaustive or analytic reference,
exposes order-dependent width, and shows the pseudo-polynomial score
frontier; it makes no production-speed claim.
Chicago ride pooling is a gated flagship application: realized co-presence is
released, while partner identity and fine geography are hidden. Current City
documentation supports a threshold-and-paired-end coarsening rule, but
complete-day estimates remain conditional on run closure, implementation
validation, and release-snapshot stability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Many public datasets preserve outcomes for individual records but remove the
key identifying which records participated in the same event. Sensitive
endpoint attributes may also be generalized or suppressed. Aggregate
quantities such as the composition of matched pairs, referral chains, or
shared-resource groups then depend on two latent objects at once: the relation
and the attributes attached to its endpoints. Selecting one linkage or deleting
rows with missing context creates precision by replacing released uncertainty
with analyst certainty.

Propagating linkage uncertainty alone does not resolve this problem. Candidate
edges can interact with latent endpoint attributes, privacy bucket counts make
attribute completions mutually dependent, and the hidden matching determines
which endpoint attributes enter the query. Separate link and attribute bounds
can therefore combine marginally admissible choices that no single released
database could have produced.

We represent the release by a set of feasible worlds. Each world jointly
specifies a hidden matching and latent endpoint attributes, satisfies every
released value and global coarsening constraint, and covers every core record
exactly once. We compute attained lower and upper endpoints of a declared
aggregate query over this set. These endpoints are conditional on the candidate
supergraph and observation operator; they are not a point reconstruction, and
the closed interval between them need not consist entirely of attainable
values.

Learning is optional and coverage-audited. An edge model supplies a
compatibility score, but node outcomes alone do not identify partner
probabilities. We restrict feasible worlds by matching-level normalized regret
and calibrate the radius on independent markets with observed relations. A
separate supergraph budget permits selected edges outside a baseline graph.
Thus learned sharpening and candidate-support sensitivity remain visible
rather than being folded into one asserted graph.

Chicago ride pooling motivates the full structure. Consider a boundary-complete,
internally consistent cohort with a positive shared-match flag and pooled chain
size two. The public record reports realized co-presence but not
the partner key. Fine geography can also be suppressed. The eventual
application bounds neighborhood-context composition among those customer
transactions. It does not infer rider socioeconomic status, preferences,
durable ties, or all shared runs.

This working version reports three completed results and artifacts. First, it defines
joint feasible-world query semantics and identifies the exact collapse to
weighted matching. Second, it develops and implements a score-aware temporal
frontier algorithm for core--buffer matching, compiled labels, active release
counts, and a global budget for omitted candidate edges; it proves endpoint and
witness recovery, the pseudo-polynomial score boundary, and a certified
outward score relaxation. A provenance-bearing compiler maps explicit DNF
release implications to replayable states without changing their projected
world set. Third, we supply independently implemented exhaustive checks, a bounded capacity
profile, and external real/synthetic relation-truth stress tests. The real
relation is deliberately reported as nonmatching rather than forced into the
model. Explicit-DNF label duplication and the vertex-separation identity are
audit and implementation devices, not knowledge-compilation novelties.
Matching-level conformal calibration is an adopted safety layer, not a novelty
claim. The full external corpus, a boundary-complete Chicago run, and
production-scale schedules remain explicit KDD submission gates.

\section{Problem and Query Semantics}
\label{sec:problem}

\paragraph{Released records and roles.}
Let $V$ index released records and let $G=(V,E)$ be a declared candidate
supergraph. Core records $C\subseteq V$ belong to the target population;
boundary-buffer records $B\subseteq V$ may be partners of core records; and
context-only records $U=V\setminus(C\cup B)$ enter privacy counts but cannot be
selected as partners. A matching $M\subseteq E$ must satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \deg_M(i)=1\quad(i\in C),\qquad
 \deg_M(i)\le1\quad(i\in B),
 \label{eq:corebuffer}
\end{equation}
and contains no buffer--buffer edge. This boundary rule lets a core record use
a counterpart immediately outside the target window without forcing every
buffer record into the target.

\paragraph{Latent attributes and release constraints.}
Each record $i$ has a finite support $\mathcal A_i$ for a latent categorical
attribute $a_i$. Supports may be singletons for released values or larger sets
for coarsened values. In the implemented reference solver, $h(i)$ assigns each
record to one release cell for this modeled attribute. The verified observation
operator supplies rational count bounds
\begin{equation}
 L_{cv}\le \sum_{i:h(i)=c}\ind\{a_i=v\}\le U_{cv}
 \quad(c,v)\in\mathcal K.
 \label{eq:counts}
\end{equation}
More specific threshold or paired-suppression logic can be compiled into the
same feasible-world representation from explicit LOW/HIGH release formulas.
The compiler requires an operator identifier, an audit reference, and exact
row-to-factor bindings; these are provenance inputs, not proof that the
external mechanism is correct. We do not infer a converse suppression rule
from missingness alone.

\paragraph{Feasible worlds and queries.}
A feasible world $F=(M,a)$ satisfies Eqs.~\eqref{eq:corebuffer}--\eqref{eq:counts},
$a_i\in\mathcal A_i$, and any declared attribute-conditioned edge rules. Let
$\F(O,G)$ denote the finite set of such worlds. For a symmetric pair function
$g$, define
the core-transaction query
\begin{equation}
 Q_C(F)=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{\{i,j\}\in M}|\{i,j\}\cap C|\,g(a_i,a_j).
 \label{eq:query}
\end{equation}
Core--core edges contribute twice and core--buffer edges once, so the
denominator remains $|C|$. Same-bin composition uses
$g(a_i,a_j)=\ind\{b(a_i)=b(a_j)\}$; signed linear or regression queries replace
$g$ by the corresponding endpoint contribution.

We report
\begin{equation}
 \underline Q=\min_{F\in\F(O,G)}Q_C(F),\qquad
 \overline Q=\max_{F\in\F(O,G)}Q_C(F).
 \label{eq:endpoints}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint semantics]
\label{prop:endpoints}
If $\F(O,G)$ is nonempty, both extrema in Eq.~\eqref{eq:endpoints} are attained.
Every feasible world's query value lies between them. If the true world
$F^\dagger$ belongs to $\F(O,G)$, its query value is covered. The proposition
does not imply that every scalar between the endpoints is attainable.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The domain is a finite set of matchings and finite attribute assignments, so a
real-valued query attains its minimum and maximum. The bracketing and coverage
claims follow from membership. Two feasible worlds can attain only two
distinct values, so finiteness alone provides no interpolation claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Observation Operators as Auditable Constraints}
\label{sec:operator}

The world set must encode what the release mechanism actually implies, not a
convenient completion rule. We separate four inputs. \emph{Record supports}
translate released or coarsened values into finite domains. \emph{Count
factors} constrain assignments across every row contributing to a privacy
cell, including context-only rows. \emph{Release factors} state the verified
direction of the mechanism: for example, a visible value may imply that a
count cleared a threshold, while a hidden value need not imply the converse.
Finally, \emph{compatibility factors} specify which attribute pairs allow an
edge. An omitted rule leaves worlds in the set; it does not silently choose a
default completion.

\section{Coupled Endpoint Computation}
\label{sec:computation}

The method must distinguish a useful baseline from the coupled problem.

\begin{lemma}[Independent-support collapse]
\label{lem:collapse}
Suppose (i) $E$ does not depend on latent attributes, (ii) the attribute domain
is Cartesian, $\prod_i\mathcal A_i$, with no cross-record count constraint, and
(iii) the query is pair additive as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:query}. Define
\begin{align*}
 \ell_{ij}&=\min_{a_i\in\mathcal A_i,a_j\in\mathcal A_j}
 |\{i,j\}\cap C|g(a_i,a_j),\\
 u_{ij}&=\max_{a_i\in\mathcal A_i,a_j\in\mathcal A_j}
 |\{i,j\}\cap C|g(a_i,a_j).
\end{align*}
Then the lower endpoint is a minimum-weight core-cover matching with weights
$\ell_{ij}/|C|$, and the upper endpoint is the corresponding maximum-weight
matching with weights $u_{ij}/|C|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any fixed matching, selected edges share no endpoint. Under a Cartesian
attribute domain, each selected edge's minimizing or maximizing endpoint
assignment can therefore be chosen independently and combined into one global
assignment. Labels of unselected nodes can be completed arbitrarily from their
nonempty supports. Optimizing the resulting edge sums over matchings gives the
stated equalities.
\end{proof}

Thus exact cover, independent missing-bin envelopes, and signed edge objectives
are weighted-matching baselines. The nonseparable problem begins when release
counts or attribute-conditioned feasibility prevent the edgewise choices in
Lemma~\ref{lem:collapse} from coexisting.

\paragraph{Why the joint world changes an endpoint.}
Consider four core records with fixed edges $ab$ and $cd$. Their supports are
$\{A,B\}$, $\{A,C\}$, $\{A,D\}$, and $\{A,E\}$, respectively. Let $A$ form
bin 0, $B,D$ bin 1, and $C,E$ bin 2. With independent supports, the same-bin
query ranges from 0 to 1: choose $(B,C,D,E)$ for the lower witness and all $A$
for the upper witness. Add the single global release constraint
$\#\{i:a_i=A\}\le2$. At most one edge can now have equal labels, so the
attained range contracts to $[0,1/2]$. Optimizing a matching range and an
attribute range separately would retain the impossible upper value 1.

Attributes can also change the relation rather than only its query value. Let
one core $c$ have support $\{A,B\}$ and two singleton buffers $x=A$ and $y=B$.
If edge $cx$ permits only $(A,A)$ and $cy$ only $(B,B)$, a count constraint
fixing $c=A$ selects $x$, while one fixing $c=B$ selects $y$. The implemented
exact oracle reproduces both examples. They isolate the interaction absent
from the independent-support baseline without invoking a privacy mechanism
that has not been verified.

\paragraph{A coupled complexity boundary.}
Fix an integer threshold $k\ge3$. Consider a paired row-release operator that
publishes both endpoint attributes of record $i$ exactly when both its pickup
and drop-off cells contain at least $k$ records; otherwise it publishes neither.
This is an abstract operator, not an assertion about the current Chicago
release.

\begin{theorem}[Paired-suppression feasibility]
\label{thm:hardness}
For every fixed $k\ge3$, deciding whether a feasible attribute assignment and
perfect matching exist under the paired operator is NP-complete, even when the
outer relation is complete bipartite, compatibility depends only on the left
record's pickup attribute, and only left pickup attributes have non-singleton
supports.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Membership in NP follows by checking assignments, cell counts, release states,
compatibility, and matching degrees. For hardness, reduce perfect
three-dimensional matching on equally sized sets $X,Y,Z$. Each $x\in X$ chooses
a latent pickup label $p_x\in Z$. For every $z$, add $k-1$ visible context
anchors to its pickup cell; all anchors share a separate already-large drop-off
cell. Visibility forces $(k-1)+|\{x:p_x=z\}|\ge k$. Since $|X|=|Z|$, every
$z$ is chosen exactly once. Give each $x$ a private low-count drop-off cell so
its two attributes remain paired-suppressed. Match $X$ perfectly to $Y$ and
allow edge $(x,y)$ under label $z$ exactly when $(x,y,z)$ is an input triple.
Feasible worlds are therefore in bijection with perfect 3D matchings. The full
construction, including the $|Z|\ge2$ padding and size bound, is in
Appendix~\ref{app:hardness}.
\end{proof}

The reduction relies on paired row suppression and arbitrary tabular
attribute-conditioned compatibility. The construction does not apply to
endpoint-wise independent suppression and does not establish hardness for a
metric-only edge rule. These boundaries must be checked, not blurred, in any
application.

\paragraph{Compiling release formulas.}
For a scalar release factor $f$, let
$N_f(a)=\sum_i\chi_{if}(a_i)$ with $\chi_{if}\in\{0,1\}$. Its temporal
automaton stores a capped count $q_f$ and a requirement
$\rho_f\in\{\varnothing,\mathsf{LOW},\mathsf{HIGH}\}$. For a pure threshold
$k$, $q_f=\min\{k,N_f^{\rm processed}\}$; closing the factor accepts
$\mathsf{LOW}$ iff $q_f<k$ and $\mathsf{HIGH}$ iff $q_f=k$. Opposing nonempty
requirements conflict. General lower/upper bounds use the smallest cap that
retains every decision boundary, including a distinct overflow state for a
binding upper bound.

For row $i$, substantive label $a$, and terminal factor predicates $T_f$, the
implemented interface accepts an explicit DNF
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_i(a)=\bigvee_{j=1}^{c_i(a)}
 \bigwedge_{(f,r)\in C_{iaj}}[T_f(N_f)=r],
 \quad r\in\{\mathsf{LOW},\mathsf{HIGH}\}.
 \label{eq:release-dnf}
\end{equation}
It replaces $a$ by one compiled copy $(a,j)$ per clause, copies all edge score,
query, and compatibility maps, and emits the clause literals as factor
requirements. The sole empty clause is canonical TRUE. Under the abstract
paired operator, visible is one HIGH--HIGH clause and hidden is the two-clause
LOW--or--LOW formula.

\begin{lemma}[Explicit-DNF release compiler]
\label{lem:capped}
The projection of feasible compiled worlds is exactly the release-augmented
world set satisfying Eq.~\eqref{eq:release-dnf}. A projected world has one
preimage for each combination of its satisfied clauses. Compilation preserves
the node/edge topology, the record-bag width for the same forget order,
score-floor feasibility, and every label- and edge-additive query; it changes label support from
$|\mathcal A_i|$ to $\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_i}c_i(a)$ and may extend factor
lifetimes and thereby increase active-factor width. Counts capped at their last
decision boundary remain exact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For completeness, choose one satisfied clause for every row and attach its
index to the substantive label. For soundness, read the clause embedded in
each compiled label; accepting factor automata satisfy every emitted literal.
The lifted maps copy feasibility, score, and query values across clause
witnesses, so projection preserves endpoints. Compilation changes no record,
edge, or action identifier. Counts on the same side of their final decision
boundary have identical future transitions, so saturation is lossless.
\end{proof}

The compiler is polynomial in the \emph{explicit} DNF and lifted output, not a
succinct arbitrary Boolean formula; exponential expansion and LOW/HIGH middle
regions are exposed interface limits.

\paragraph{Temporal frontier.}
A supplied forget order is compiled into node-introduction, edge-processing,
and node-forget actions. Every edge is processed while both endpoints are
live; a record is forgotten only after all incident edges; and factor $f$ is
implicitly opened before its first possible contributor or requirer and closed
immediately after its last. Let $w$ be the maximum live-record count, $d$ the
largest \emph{compiled} label support (including release-witness copies),
$B_t$ the live record bag, and $\mathcal A_t$ the active factor set at action
$t$. Factor $f$ retains counts
$0,\ldots,K_f$.

For allowed label-pair scores $s_e(u,v)$, set
\[
 a=\min\left\{0,\min_{e,(u,v)\in\mathcal P_e}s_e(u,v)\right\}.
\]
Every world has total selected core
incidence $N=|C|$, so replacing $s_e$ by $s_e-a\ge0$ adds the same $-aN$ to
every score. Clear rational denominators and cap the accumulated integer score
at the transformed floor $b$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact score-aware temporal-frontier endpoints]
\label{thm:temporal}
For a valid schedule, omission budget $\Gamma$, rational additive query, and
score floor, the temporal-frontier dynamic program returns infeasible exactly
when the declared world set is empty; otherwise it returns both attained query
endpoints and witness worlds, provided the declared frontier-record limit is
not exceeded. Exceeding that operational limit raises an explicit frontier-limit
exception and licenses no feasibility conclusion. Before dominance pruning, the number of
live keys at action $t$ are at most
\begin{equation}
 \left(\prod_{i\in B_t}m_i d'_i\right)(\Gamma+1)(b+1)
 \prod_{f\in\mathcal A_t}3(K_f+1),
\label{eq:statebound}
\end{equation}
where $d'_i=\sum_{a\in\mathcal A_i}c_i(a)$, $m_i=2$ for matchable records and
$m_i=1$ for context-only records. If $d'_i\le d$, $|B_t|\le w$, all active
pure thresholds have cap $k$, and at most $r$ are active, this is at most
$(2d)^w[3(k+1)]^r(\Gamma+1)(b+1)$. Without a score floor the factor $(b+1)$
disappears.
The work per transition is polynomial in the explicit state and input size.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
At action $t$, a key stores live labels, live matching bits, the
$(q_f,\rho_f)$ automaton for each $f\in\mathcal A_t$, used omitted edges, and
the score capped at $b$. For each key the program keeps separate exact minimum
and maximum accumulated query values. These fields contain every processed
quantity on which a future transition can depend. The running-intersection
conditions ensure that forgotten labels and finalized factors can never be
consulted again. Induction over actions therefore shows that the retained
values are exactly the extrema among partial worlds inducing the key. Terminal
paths have every core matched, every factor finalized, and score state $b$;
they project onto precisely the feasible full worlds. Stored choices recover
the two witnesses. Counting each live label, matching bit, factor automaton,
and global resource gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:statebound}.
\end{proof}

For a forget order $\pi=(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$, define the forward boundary
$\partial^+_\pi(i)=\{v_j:j>i,\ \exists h\le i:\{v_h,v_j\}\in E\}$. The
compiler's largest record bag is exactly
$1+\max_i|\partial^+_\pi(i)|$, the reverse-order vertex-separation number plus
one; minimizing over orders yields pathwidth \citep{kinnersley1992vertex}.
Hence, if records are ordered by nondecreasing
time and every candidate edge spans at most $\Delta$, then
$w\le\max_t|\{i:t\le\tau_i\le t+\Delta\}|$. Active-factor width is likewise
the maximum overlap of the compiler-audited factor lifecycle intervals. These
are data-measurable certificates for a supplied order, not a claim that the
order minimizes pathwidth.

An exact algorithm polynomial in binary input length at fixed structural width
would imply $P=NP$; our dynamic program pays a pseudo-polynomial score coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[Weak hardness at pathwidth two]
\label{thm:scorehard}
For nonnegative integer score and query contributions encoded in binary,
deciding whether a perfect matching has score at least $B$ and additive query
at most $B$ is weakly NP-complete even with singleton labels, no release
factors, $\Gamma=0$, and a candidate graph that is a disjoint union of
four-cycles.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Reduce \textsc{Subset Sum}. For item $a_i$, a four-cycle has two perfect
matchings, ``take'' and ``skip''. Give one take edge score $a_i$ per core
incidence and query $2a_i$; all other contributions are zero. With both
thresholds $2B$, a global matching is feasible exactly when the taken items
sum to $B$. Each component, and their concatenation, has pathwidth two.
Membership in NP is immediate. This is the model's pseudo-polynomial boundary,
consistent with known budgeted-matching hardness and approximation results
\citep{berger2011budgeted,doronarad2023eptas}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Certified score relaxation.}
When $b$ is too large, choose rational $\eta>0$, set
$r_e=\lfloor(s_e-a)/\eta\rfloor$, and impose
\[
 \widehat b=\max\{0,\lceil(B-Na)/\eta\rceil-N\}.
\]
\begin{proposition}[Outward score certificate]
\label{prop:relax}
If $\F_B$ is the exact score-floor set and $\widehat\F_\eta$ the rounded set,
then
\begin{equation}
 \F_B\subseteq\widehat\F_\eta\subseteq\F_{B-\eta N}.
 \label{eq:relaxation}
\end{equation}
Consequently, optimizing over $\widehat\F_\eta$ gives outward query endpoints.
Each returned witness misses the original score floor by at most $\eta N$;
if an endpoint witness satisfies the original floor, that endpoint is exact.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $c_e=|e\cap C|$ for selected-edge core incidence and suppress the
selected label-pair argument of $s_e$ and $r_e$. Fixed core incidence gives
$\eta\sum c_er_e\le\sum c_e(s_e-a)
<\eta(\sum c_er_e+N)$, which proves both inclusions after integer rounding.
Set inclusion gives the endpoint order. If a relaxed extremal witness is also
exact-feasible, the reverse endpoint inequality follows from that witness.
\end{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:relaxation} is a bicriteria score certificate, not a query
FPTAS: a tiny score slack may cause an arbitrary query jump.

\paragraph{Reference formulation.}
The implemented coupled reference program uses binary edge variables $z_e$,
assignment variables $x_{iv}$, and joint edge-label variables $y_{euv}$ only
for ordered pairs $(u,v)$ in an edge's declared compatibility set
$\mathcal P_e$. It enforces Eq.~\eqref{eq:corebuffer},
$\sum_{v\in\mathcal A_i}x_{iv}=1$, the count bounds in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:counts}, and
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \sum_{(u,v)\in\mathcal P_e}y_{euv}&=z_e,\\
 y_{euv}&\le x_{iu},\qquad y_{euv}\le x_{jv},\\
 y_{euv}&\ge z_e+x_{iu}+x_{jv}-2.
 \end{aligned}
\]
The query is linear in $y$. Context-only records have assignment variables and
enter counts but have no incident selectable edge. An exhaustive enumerator is
the exact oracle on small instances; the sparse mixed-integer program is a
numerical reference backend. It also enforces optional score floors and
supergraph-edge budgets. Exact optima, numerical optima, exact or structural
infeasibility, numerical infeasibility, and unresolved runs are reported as
distinct states; a timeout is unresolved rather than a separate certificate.

The separate temporal implementation accepts tuple-valued compiled labels,
multiple label-dependent count contributions and release requirements, and
label-pair-specific scores and queries. It constructs sparse active-factor
coordinates from a supplied forget order, uses exact rational arithmetic, and
independently replays each endpoint witness. The high-level compiler implements
Lemma~\ref{lem:capped}, records support expansion, validates node/edge/factor
lifecycles, and restores the selected DNF clause from a projected witness. Its
mandatory audit reference does not verify an external operator, and no
Chicago-specific null-cause adapter is claimed. The Python reference stores
complete partial witnesses rather than memory-optimal predecessor pointers;
Eq.~\eqref{eq:statebound} is its key-count bound, not a production runtime
claim. General MILP remains the fallback when the measured temporal frontier
is too wide.

\section{Learning-Augmented Restrictions}
\label{sec:learning}

\subsection{What Node Outcomes Do Not Identify}

Let $\partial M\in\{0,1\}^{V}$ be the incidence vector of a matching. A
released match indicator can reveal $\partial M$ without revealing $M$.

\begin{proposition}[Partner nonidentification]
\label{prop:nonidentification}
If a candidate graph contains distinct matchings $M_1$ and $M_2$ with
$\partial M_1=\partial M_2$, then node match outcomes and node covariates alone
do not identify which of those relations occurred. In particular, an
all-positive incidence vector does not distinguish any two perfect matchings.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take two latent worlds with the same released nodes, covariates, and incidence
vector, but relation $M_1$ in one and $M_2$ in the other. Their observations
coincide while their partner edges differ. No function of the released data can
distinguish them without another model or source of relation truth.
\end{proof}

This result does not prohibit structured weak supervision; it fixes its claim
boundary. A model may score complete matchings and enforce mutual exclusion,
but a node-level objective alone cannot validate partner ranks in the regime
of Proposition~\ref{prop:nonidentification}. In our controlled benchmark,
edge scorers are therefore trained on separate synthetic source markets whose
relations are observed. Chicago node outcomes are not used as hidden edge
labels.

\subsection{Calibrating a Matching Set}

Let $s_e$ be a matching-only scorer fixed and deterministically rationalized
before calibration. With optional buffer nodes, use core incidence,
\begin{equation}
 S_C(M)=\sum_{e\in M}|e\cap C|s_e.
 \label{eq:corescore}
\end{equation}
Because $\sum_{e\in M}|e\cap C|=|C|$, replacing $s_e$ by $as_e+b$ for $a>0$
changes every feasible score by the same additive constant after scaling.
For market $d$, let $h_d(M)$ count selected edges in
$E_d^{\rm super}\setminus E_d^{\rm base}$ and define
\[
 \F^0_{d,\Gamma}=\{F\in\F(O_d,G_d^{\rm super}):h_d(M(F))\le\Gamma\}.
\]
Before calibration, fix a reference budget $\bar\Gamma_d$ by the same rule in
every market and compute once
\[
 S^-_{d,\star}=\min_{F\in\F^0_{d,\bar\Gamma_d}}S_C(M(F)),\qquad
 S^+_{d,\star}=\max_{F\in\F^0_{d,\bar\Gamma_d}}S_C(M(F)).
\]
These extrema are frozen along the entire $\Gamma$ path. Define
\begin{equation}
 R_{d,\star}(F)=
 \frac{S^+_{d,\star}-S_C(M(F))}{S^+_{d,\star}-S^-_{d,\star}},
 \label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
with value zero for a constant reference score. Recomputing the normalizer at
each $\Gamma$ can destroy nesting: a newly admitted high-score world can raise
the floor enough to delete an old world.

For any $\tau\in[0,1]$ and $\Gamma\le\bar\Gamma_d$, define
\begin{equation}
 \F_{d,\tau,\Gamma}
 =\left\{F\in\F^0_{d,\Gamma}:
 S_C(M(F))\ge S^+_{d,\star}
 -\tau(S^+_{d,\star}-S^-_{d,\star})\right\}.
 \label{eq:calset}
\end{equation}
Use $m$ calibration markets, disjoint from scorer training, whose true
matchings are observed. For score miscoverage $\alpha_S$, let
$k_S=\lceil(m+1)(1-\alpha_S)\rceil$ and let $\widehat\tau_{\alpha_S}$ be the
$k_S$th smallest calibration regret, using the support bound one if
$k_S=m+1$. Retain the set in Eq.~\eqref{eq:calset} at
$\tau=\widehat\tau_{\alpha_S}$.
Exact additive scores parse each component through its declared decimal
spelling before rational termwise summation; the same score map is used for
calibration and the endpoint solver. A
label-dependent scorer would instead require calibration truth for the full
world, not only its matching.

\begin{theorem}[Market-level score retention and query coverage]
\label{thm:conformal}
Suppose the scorer, observation operator, graph rule, reference budgets, and
normalization are fixed before calibration. Suppose the augmented markets,
including their true worlds, are exchangeable, every reference set is
nonempty, and
$F_d^\dagger\in\F^0_{d,\bar\Gamma_d}$ almost surely. Then
\[
 \Pr\{R_{m+1,\star}(F^\dagger_{m+1})\le\widehat\tau_{\alpha_S}\}
 \ge1-\alpha_S.
\]
For a reported $\Gamma\le\bar\Gamma_{m+1}$, if
$\Pr\{F^\dagger_{m+1}\notin\F^0_{m+1,\Gamma}\}\le\alpha_G$, then
\[
 \Pr\{F^\dagger_{m+1}\in
 \F_{m+1,\widehat\tau_{\alpha_S},\Gamma}\}
 \ge1-\alpha_S-\alpha_G.
\]
On this event, every query range optimized over that same set covers its true
value.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Exchangeability gives the usual split-conformal rank bound. A union bound with
the final-$\Gamma$ eligibility failure gives the second display; independence
is unnecessary. Conditional on true-world retention,
Proposition~\ref{prop:endpoints} covers all queries sharing that world set, so
no union bound over queries is needed.
\end{proof}

A supplied supergraph or synthetic calibration alone establishes neither rate
for Chicago. With the frozen reference range, the two sensitivities do form a
nested audit rather than one opaque knob.

\begin{proposition}[Nested sensitivity paths]
\label{prop:nested}
If $\tau\le\tau'$ and $\Gamma\le\Gamma'\le\bar\Gamma_d$, then
$\F_{d,\tau,\Gamma}\subseteq\F_{d,\tau',\Gamma'}$. Consequently, a query's
lower endpoint cannot increase and its upper endpoint cannot decrease when
either budget is relaxed.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Both structural budgets and one fixed score halfspace are nested. Optimization
over the resulting superset gives the endpoint monotonicity.
\end{proof}

Reporting the $(\tau,\Gamma)$ path exposes whether apparent precision comes
from score or candidate exclusion; it does not turn either into graph-support
evidence.

\section{Chicago Flagship Application: Evidence Gate}
\label{sec:chicago}

Chicago's public Transportation Network Provider table reports whether each
customer transaction shared a vehicle with a separately booked customer and
reports the pooled run size \citep{chicagodata2026,chicagomanual2026}. The
provider-side Shared Trip ID groups the empty-to-empty vehicle run but is absent
from the public schema. Conditional on run closure and reporting consistency,
records with match true and $K=2$ therefore induce a hidden nonbipartite
transaction matching. This is neither a passenger count nor a claim that
$K>2$ runs form co-presence cliques.

The current base dataset metadata points to the City's privacy methodology as
the approach used in this release. That note lists the current dataset and
documents an at-most-two-unique-trip threshold for 15-minute tract cells with
paired coarsening of both endpoints \citep{chicagoprivacy2026}. A companion
clarification describes separate pickup/start and drop-off/end cells
\citep{chicagoclarification2026}. We therefore compile the safe one-way
implication: a published fine tract rules out a low-count applicable internal
cell at either endpoint. We do \emph{not} infer LOW from a blank tract; the City
also lists missing source geography and outside-city locations. Every trip
contributing to a relevant cell, not only the matched cohort, must enter the
count factors. Documentary support is not an audit of the City's code,
provider partitioning, DST handling, or late-row recomputation.

The production analysis is still not licensed. The committed data are a
deterministic two-character trip-ID-prefix sample, which omits a retained
record's counterpart in approximately 255 out of 256 cases under
independent prefix retention. Even an authorized-only complete day does not
prove run closure: a partner can cross midnight or released flags can be
inconsistent. The City may publish after generally 99\% or more expected data
have arrived and later revise past periods \citep{chicagoupdates2026}; hence
``complete day'' means complete only within a pinned snapshot and requires a
later stabilization check.

The complete application requires all transactions contributing to each
privacy cell, run-closed boundary buffers, a necessary-condition candidate
supergraph, the correct tract vintage, and full solver status by component.
Primary comparisons will include complete cases, community-area aggregation,
independent attribute envelopes, one point imputation, spatiotemporal proxy
grouping, score-free coupled endpoints, and calibrated restrictions only where
independent partner truth supports them. No Chicago composition or January
2026 policy sign is reported in this draft.

\section{Limitations and Ethical Considerations}

The endpoint guarantee is conditional on the declared world set. Candidate
omissions, an incorrect release operator, nonexchangeable calibration markets,
or unmodeled attribute supports can remove truth before optimization. The
split-conformal result is marginal across complete markets, not conditional on
every market and not transferable from simulation by assertion. Numerical
MILP optima are not exact mathematical certificates; production reporting must
retain primal/dual gaps and unresolved states.

The Chicago unit is a customer transaction, not a stable person. Census
attributes describe neighborhood context, not rider income or race. Releasing
one extremal latent assignment could facilitate reidentification, so the
intended artifact reports only aggregate endpoints, support diagnostics, and
solver certificates. It must not publish imputed partner links, trip IDs, or
latent fine-geography assignments. Institutional review and the City's data
terms must be resolved before any nonpublic data are used.

\section{Generative AI Usage}

Generative AI assisted code drafting, adversarial counterexample search, and
language editing. Every mathematical claim, experiment, citation, and code path
remains subject to author verification. No generated text or synthetic output
is treated as evidence about Chicago.

\section{Conclusion}

Privacy coarsening can hide a relation and its endpoint attributes at the same
time. The appropriate output is then a query over feasible worlds, not a
preferred reconstructed graph. Independent attribute supports reduce to
weighted matching; active release factors create the coupled problem. The
temporal-frontier algorithm gives exact endpoints when live structural width
and the score resource are controlled, while the outward relaxation remains
honest when the exact score frontier is too large. Matching-level conformal
calibration is a known safety layer that can expose false precision, not the
algorithmic contribution. The remaining work is decisive: verify and compile
the City implementation against complete snapshots, construct and measure a
run-closed full-day temporal order, scan the full external corpus, and run the
Chicago application.

\section{Full Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:hardness}}
\label{app:hardness}

Define the decision problem as follows. Records have pickup and drop-off time
labels, finite attribute supports, and an observed symbol that is either a
visible attribute pair or missing. A world chooses both attributes. The paired
operator releases both exactly when both selected endpoint cells have count at
least $k$. Target records form left and right sets and must be perfectly
matched; context records cannot be matched. An explicitly represented table
$\Gamma_{xy}(p_x)$ determines compatibility. A certificate is polynomially
checked by computing all counts and matching degrees, so the problem is in NP.

Reduce perfect 3D matching with $|X|=|Y|=|Z|=q$. If needed, add one disjoint
forced triple so $q\ge2$ without changing the answer. Create target records
$r_x$ and $r_y$ and the complete outer graph $X\times Y$. All $r_x$ share one
pickup time and have support $Z$. Each $r_x$ has a private drop-off time and a
singleton drop-off support; its observed pair is missing. Each $r_y$ has
private endpoint times, singleton supports, and a missing observed pair.

For each $z\in Z$, create $k-1$ context anchors with the common pickup time and
singleton pickup support $\{z\}$. All anchors share a drop-off time and a
singleton label unused by targets, and are visibly released. The anchor
drop-off count is $q(k-1)\ge k$. If $n_z=|\{x:p_x=z\}|$, each $z$-anchor pickup
count is $(k-1)+n_z$; visibility is therefore equivalent to $n_z\ge1$.
Because $\sum_z n_z=q$, every $n_z=1$. Missing target outputs are consistent:
every $x$ has a unique count-one drop-off cell, and every $y$ has unique
count-one endpoint cells.

Finally set $\Gamma_{xy}(z)=1$ exactly for input triples $(x,y,z)$. A feasible
world matches every $x$ and $y$ once and assigns every $z$ once, so its selected
triples form a perfect 3D matching. Conversely, a perfect 3D matching selects
the corresponding $p_x$ and $(x,y)$ edges; all anchor counts equal or exceed
$k$, all targets remain suppressed, and every selected edge is compatible.
For fixed $k$ the construction has $O(q)$ records, $O(q^2)$ support and outer-
edge entries, and at most an $O(q^3)$ explicit compatibility table. The
reduction is polynomial.

\section{Proof Details for Theorem~\ref{thm:temporal}}

Let $X_t$ be a live key after action $t$ and let $\mathcal W_t(X_t)$ be the
partial compiled worlds that induce its live labels and matching bits, active
capped counts and merged requirements, used omission budget, and capped score.
They have already satisfied every forgotten-core degree constraint and every
closed factor. For endpoint sense $\circ\in\{\min,\max\}$, the invariant is
\[
 V_t^\circ(X_t)=\mathop{\circ}_{F\in\mathcal W_t(X_t)}H_t(F),
\]
with the value attained by the stored partial witness.

The empty layer satisfies the invariant. A node introduction enumerates every
compiled label, increments precisely its active factor automata, merges its
requirements, and rejects exactly conflicts or factor states with no feasible
completion. An edge action enumerates skip and select; select is legal exactly
when both matching bits are zero and the live labels form an allowed pair, and
it updates the two global resources. A forget action checks a core's bit and
removes data whose final incident edge and factor have already been processed.
Closing a factor applies its terminal predicate and removes its automaton.
Thus every transition is a legal extension and every legal extension is
enumerated.

By the schedule's running-intersection property, two partial worlds with the
same key have identical future extensions and future additive contributions.
Keeping only their attained minimum and maximum is therefore lossless.
Induction proves the invariant at every layer. Terminal accepting paths have
all core degrees one, all factors closed, omission count at most $\Gamma$, and
capped score $b$; conversely every feasible full world traces such a path.
Backtracking yields the two endpoint witnesses.

For live record $i$, there are $m_id'_i$ label/bit assignments;
active factor $f$ has at most $3(K_f+1)$ automaton states, alongside
$\Gamma+1$ omission and $b+1$ capped-score states. Their product gives
Eq.~\eqref{eq:statebound}; each explicit transition performs polynomial work.
The reference implementation copies complete partial witnesses for auditability,
whereas a predecessor-pointer implementation attains the usual rolling-state
storage bound.

\section{Full Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:scorehard}}

Membership in NP follows by checking a proposed perfect matching and its two
integer sums. Reduce \textsc{Subset Sum} with positive integers
$a_1,\ldots,a_m$ and target $B$. For each item create a cycle
$u_i-v_i-x_i-y_i-u_i$ with all four vertices core. Its only perfect matchings
are
\[
 M_i^{\rm take}=\{u_iv_i,x_iy_i\},\qquad
 M_i^{\rm skip}=\{v_ix_i,y_iu_i\}.
\]
Assign $s_{u_iv_i}=a_i$ and zero score to the other three edges. Since the
edge is core--core, taking contributes $2a_i$ to the core-incidence score.
Give the same take edge query $2a_i$, with every other query zero. A global
perfect matching independently chooses take or skip in each cycle and hence
has score and query both equal to $2\sum_{i\in I}a_i$. Requiring score at least
$2B$ and query at most $2B$ is therefore equivalent to
$\sum_{i\in I}a_i=B$.

Each cycle has the width-two path decomposition
\[
 \{u_i,v_i,y_i\},\qquad \{v_i,x_i,y_i\}.
\]
Decompositions of disjoint components can
be concatenated without increasing width. The associated decision problem is
pseudo-polynomially solvable by the score-frontier DP, so it is weakly
NP-complete.
