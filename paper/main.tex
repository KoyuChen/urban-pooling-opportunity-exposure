\title{Visible Rows, Hidden Events: Certified Aggregates for Relation-Incomplete Event Streams}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Many event streams are row-complete but relation-incomplete: every transaction
is visible, yet the key joining rows into shared referrals, visits, service
episodes, or vehicle runs is suppressed. Choosing one linkage can turn this
structural uncertainty into apparently precise but decision-unstable
statistics. We introduce \methodname, which optimizes aggregate queries over
all temporally ordered event decompositions consistent with released rows,
timestamp supports, and a declared simultaneous-capacity bound. An event need
not be a clique: sequential members may form an $A$--$B$--$C$ overlap chain
although $A$ and $C$ never coexist. We prove joint nesting under timestamp
support expansion and capacity relaxation. For a fixed root and span,
connectivity and occupancy have a consecutive-ones formulation, yielding an
integral LP pricing oracle. Dantzig--Wolfe decomposition closes the full LP;
branching on row usage and Ryan--Foster co-membership, while reusing exact
branch-compatible pricing, gives a certified integer algorithm. In 3,000
controlled-truth experiments, full candidate support covers the true aggregate
in every instance, while a feasible temporal point rule makes threshold errors
in 17.4--19.0\% of cases; every such error is flagged by a frontier crossing the
threshold. Retaining about 84\% of true members leaves the complete true event
world representable only 31--33\% of the time. A predeclared NYC audit yields 21
eligible windows and 126 outcome--capacity cells: 125 are certified ambiguous
at the candidate-median threshold, and four feasible point methods disagree in
19.0\% of cells. Branch-and-price certifies 14 of 18 predeclared scale cells and
reports valid gaps for the rest. A Chicago audit count-closes 60 cores, 611
temporal candidates, and 50,405 endpoint-bin contributors. These are
conditional feasible-world claims, not recovered operational event identities,
realized capacities, or population effects.
\end{abstract}
